# V2 evidence, corrections and limits

Checked 16 October 2026. This file explains the decisions in [V2](RECIPES_V2.md). All figures are for the named product/version; check the pack you actually buy. This is food planning, not personalised clinical nutrition advice.

## Evidence behind the design

| Decision | Evidence and interpretation | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Keep vegetables, legumes, wholegrains, nuts, fish/tofu and unsaturated oils as the foundation | [Australian Dietary Guidelines](https://www.eatforhealth.gov.au/guidelines/australian-dietary-guidelines-1-5) support a varied nutritious pattern. | They do not rank particular brands or certify this recipe as optimal. |
| Keep vegetable diversity; use frozen vegetables to make it practical | [Eat for Health: vegetables and legumes](https://www.eatforhealth.gov.au/food-essentials/five-food-groups/vegetables-and-legumes-beans) includes fresh, frozen, canned and dried options. | More bulk than you can comfortably eat is not the goal. |
| Include a measured oily-fish rotation | [Heart Foundation omega-3 guidance](https://www.heartfoundation.org.au/healthy-living/healthy-eating/omega-3-omega-6-heart-health) recommends 2–3 fish servings weekly and distinguishes marine omega-3 from plant ALA. [Fish article](https://www.heartfoundation.org.au/blog/not-eating-enough-fish) gives 100 g canned fish as a serving. | V2's eight 100 g portions cover two weekly serves only if eaten over four weeks. Other meals and the actual eating schedule matter. |
| Prefer olive oil/nuts as part of the overall pattern | The [2018 republished PREDIMED trial](https://www.nejm.org/doi/abs/10.1056/NEJMoa1800389) studied Mediterranean dietary patterns with supplied olive oil/nuts in 7,447 older, high-cardiovascular-risk adults. | This is not a trial of these freezer meals or younger adults. The [original report was retracted and republished because of randomisation irregularities](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMc1806491). Do not translate its findings into years of life gained from lunch. |
| Use fewer salty sauces; flavour with lemon, garlic and spices | Australian Dietary Guidelines advise limiting added salt and saturated fat. | Some sodium occurs naturally or in canned fish. No whole-bowl sodium total has been verified. |
| Make cooling explicit | [NSW leftovers guidance](https://www.foodauthority.nsw.gov.au/consumer/food-extra-care/leftovers) covers prompt chilling, storage, reheating and the cumulative 2/4-hour rule. [NSW cooling benchmark](https://www.foodauthority.nsw.gov.au/retail/standard-322a-food-safety-management-tools/showing-food-safe) supplies the temperature checkpoints. | A domestic fridge may not safely cool twenty warm large portions at once. The 75°C reheating target in V2 is conservative; the cited consumer page specifies steaming hot and at least 60°C. |

## Corrections to earlier material

1. **Fish frequency:** six small tins do not automatically provide six 100 g fish servings; the earlier September calculation also assumed ten work lunches over two weeks. Extending that arithmetic to twenty lunches was misleading. V2 measures 100 g drained fish per designated fish meal and states the calendar assumption.
2. **Tofu calcium:** the ingredient name alone is insufficient. Use a declared calcium value or call it unknown. V2 offers both a declared-calcium product and an honest budget alternative.
3. **Greens:** V1's blanket statement that both spinach and kale do not count as calcium sources is too broad. V2 avoids that claim and uses quantified tofu/fortified-milk values rather than treating greens as the entire calcium plan.
4. **Plant milk:** product names are not interchangeable. Soy, almond and oat versions have different labels; an oat product may contain gluten. Additives and fortification are not automatically a reason to reject a nutritionally useful food.
5. **Shopping totals:** 190 g walnuts cannot supply twenty 10 g portions; 3 kg oranges does not guarantee twenty fruit; twenty 250 mL drinks require 5 L. Pantry purchases and already-owned ingredients must be separated.
6. **Fibre tolerance:** cutting grain is not a general solution to discomfort from a very legume-heavy bowl. Portion size, legume amount and individual tolerance matter.
7. **Scores:** V1's 86–96/100 scores are historical subjective judgements, not validated nutrition outcomes. V2 does not manipulate the scoring system to claim perfection.
8. **Nutrition totals:** final cooked yield, label discrepancies and protein variations prevent an honest exact total at this stage. Specific tofu/drink figures are verified label calculations; whole-bowl energy, fibre and sodium are not. Never apply a ten-portion estimate to eighteen portions.
