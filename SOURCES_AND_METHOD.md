# Sources, methods, health caveats and food safety

_Reviewed 16 October 2026._

## Scope and status definitions

This repository is a practical archive of meals designed or prepared at home. It is **not** a medical record or a controlled scientific experiment.

- **Observed cooked / prepared:** dated photographs of filled containers, an explicit report of actual yield, or strong contemporaneous cooking notes.
- **Supported cook-up:** dated photographs of meal-prep setup paired with a matching recipe, but an actual finished-container count may be unverified.
- **Planned:** a saved recipe, shopping list or meal roster, with no independent evidence that it was made.
- **Version/superseded:** an earlier proposal subsequently edited; do not conflate quantities or rankings with the final batch.
- **Exact recipe not recovered:** preserve what is known and state what is missing instead of filling gaps by assumption.

Container evidence supports preparation but **does not establish consumption on particular dates**. The reconstructed dates of eating in the historical “History of meals” document largely illustrate a model of one meal a day rather than a verified food diary. A family meal roster entry is also evidence of a plan, not proof of eating.

## The /100 historic scores

Historical figures are from an earlier rubric combining ten subjective categories (/10 each): vegetable variety; legumes/fibre; carbohydrate quality; protein adequacy; protein source; unsaturated fat/omega-3; sodium; added sugar/processing; micronutrient breadth; and satiety/portion balance. These categories were assigned by analysis of recipe components, **not** by validated clinical scores or laboratory nutrient assays. Scores may have been overconfident, and earlier version scores must not transfer automatically to a changed recipe or different portion yield.

Useful questions for future comparisons: How many grams of vegetables and pulses per serving? Is protein adequate for a full lunch? How much seafood and calcium are eaten across the **whole week**? Is the planned portion sufficient for activity level? Is sodium high? Does it taste good enough to keep making?

**Per-portion nutrients** are approximate unless calculated from weighed cooked yields, drained package weights and current Australian nutrition-panel data. Avoid implying the August, September or October recipes directly improved HRV, weight, resting heart rate or longevity. The accompanying observational wearable study explicitly did not establish causation.

- [Australian Government standard food-group serves](https://www.eatforhealth.gov.au/food-essentials/how-much-do-we-need-each-day/serve-sizes)
- [Heart Foundation: heart-healthy eating pattern](https://www.heartfoundation.org.au/healthy-living/healthy-eating/heart-healthy-eating-pattern)
- [Heart Foundation: protein and heart health](https://www.heartfoundation.org.au/healthy-living/healthy-eating/protein-and-heart-health)
- [Heart Foundation: fats, oils and heart health](https://www.heartfoundation.org.au/healthy-living/healthy-eating/fats-oils-and-heart-health)

Strong recurring features: legumes, vegetables and fruit, whole/high-fibre grains, fish or tofu, nuts/seeds, predominantly unsaturated fats, reduced reliance on salty sauces and processed meat. Nutrient variety across weeks matters more than maximising ingredient count in a single container. If a dairy-free diet excludes ordinary milk/yoghurt, arrange reliable calcium sources such as fortified soy drinks, calcium-set tofu or fish with edible bones; verify relevant labels.

## Food safety: this supersedes older archived instructions

- Split cooked rice, lentils, fish, egg and other cooked foods promptly into **shallow, meal-sized containers**.
- Do not deliberately leave big batches sitting for hours on a bench. Refrigerate promptly with airflow so portions can cool, then seal/label/freeze when cold.
- As a useful benchmark, [Food Standards Australia New Zealand (FSANZ)](https://www.foodstandards.gov.au/business/food-safety/cooling-and-reheating-food) specifies cooling potentially hazardous foods from 60°C to 21°C within **2 hours**, then 21°C to 5°C within another **4 hours**. These are food-business benchmarks; for home meal prep, faster shallow-portion refrigeration is sensible.
- Thaw in the fridge or by microwave as directed; reheat once thoroughly so the centre is piping/steaming hot. If using a thermometer, aim for about **75°C**.
- Rice in particular can pose a *Bacillus cereus* hazard when left warm; freezing cannot undo toxins already produced.
- Check product labels for dairy and gluten claims (especially sauces, spices, soy milk and processed items). Do not assume all editions of a named brand are allergen-free.
- Don't invent freezer storage durations or safe temperatures from appearance alone. Follow official storage recommendations and safe handling.

## Public-repository privacy rule

Do not upload household photos, individual health records, medical histories, identity documents, financial transactions or wearable raw data here. Only recipe-level information and explicit source references. Cooking plans should be shared without names of children or private family scheduling.
