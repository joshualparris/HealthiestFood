# V5: four varied, nutrient-dense freezer recipes for Dubbo

**16 October 2026 · 20 adult lunches · four batches of five · dairy-free ingredients, subject to labels.**

[Shopping and prices](SHOPPING.md) · [Evidence and nutrition assumptions](EVIDENCE.md) · [Reproducible calculation](calculate.py)

V5 is a new design, not a kitchen-tested recipe collection or a scientifically proven optimum. V2 and the October Woolworths optimisation were inspected first. V3 was published during this work; its recipes and nutrition audit were also read before V5 publication. V4 was not yet available in the inspected main-branch index. Older recipes are retained. Compared with V3, V5 offers four smaller batches, declared-calcium tofu, smaller measured nut toppings, a plain-salmon option and an explicit energy/protein model; it does not prove a greater health benefit.

## What is genuinely different

- **Four flavours and smaller cook-ups:** Mediterranean sardine; lemon salmon and greens; ginger lentil curry; smoky black-bean tofu. Five meals at once are easier to cool than twenty, though fridge capacity still matters.
- **Measured protein and moderate grain portions:** 30 g dry grain per lunch, plus legumes, fish or 150 g tofu. No ever-growing pile of seeds or powders.
- **An explicit sodium/calcium trade-off:** plain salmon has less sodium than the selected canned salmon, but costs more and lacks canned bones. Sardines and declared-calcium tofu remain in the rotation.
- **A transparent energy/protein estimate:** ingredient assumptions and arithmetic are supplied. These are planning numbers, not verified nutrient panels.
- **No false perfect score:** variety and practical execution improve the plan; V5 is not proven to extend life more than V2.

## Common measures, equipment and labels

Weights are **edible weights**: washed vegetables after necessary trimming, drained beans/fish, dry grains/lentils, raw salmon and drained tofu. Use metric teaspoons (**5 mL**). No Australian/US tablespoon ambiguity.

You need kitchen scales, a fine sieve, a large saucepan, a second pot for grain, one or two baking trays, shallow freezer containers and ideally a probe thermometer. Start with five **750 mL–1 L** containers per recipe; if food would form a deep layer, split a meal between two shallow containers. Do not force a predetermined final weight: cooking changes water content. Weigh each complete cooked component and divide by five.

Preferred tofu is **Pureland High Protein Firm Tofu 500 g**, not the ordinary product. Buy three packs: 750 g for C and 750 g for D. It declares 120 mg calcium and 17.9 g protein/100 g, providing about **180 mg calcium and 27 g protein per tofu portion**, before other ingredients. Check the pack; Macro tofu is a useful cheaper alternative but has no quantified calcium in the retrieved panel.

All recipes contain potential allergens: fish in A/B, soy in C/D, walnuts at serving. Check dairy/gluten cross-contact statements on actual packs if strict avoidance is needed. Naturally gluten-free ingredients do not establish a certified gluten-free meal.

## A. Lemon-paprika sardine, chickpea and quinoa bowls

**Five meals · about 50–65 minutes preparation/cooking, plus cooling.**

### Exact ingredients

- 8 × 110 g John West Sardines in Springwater / retailer-labelled Salt Reduced tins: expected **600 g drained total**, about 120 g per meal. Actual drained yield governs; keep soft edible bones.
- **150 g dry quinoa**.
- **500 g drained/rinsed no-added-salt chickpeas**.
- **400 g** no-added-salt diced tomatoes with juice.
- **400 g** frozen plain broccoli/cauliflower.
- **250 g** carrots, cut into 1 cm slices.
- **200 g** red capsicum, deseeded and chopped.
- **200 g** zucchini, thick half-moons.
- **100 g** peeled onion, diced.
- **150 g** spinach.
- **25 mL extra-virgin olive oil**: 15 mL for roasting, 10 mL for sauce.
- **10 g** peeled garlic, minced; **2 tsp cumin**, **2 tsp smoked paprika**, **1 tsp oregano**, **¼ tsp pepper**.
- **40 mL lemon juice**, optional zest of half a washed lemon.
- **100–200 mL water** for sauce as needed.
- **50 g unsalted walnuts**, separate, 10 g per serving.
- No added salt initially; fish already supplies sodium.

### Method

1. Heat oven to **190°C fan**. Toss carrot, capsicum and zucchini with 15 mL oil and half the cumin/paprika. Spread on a tray; roast **25–35 minutes**, turning once, until tender and lightly browned. Avoid burnt edges.
2. Rinse quinoa thoroughly. Cook to packet directions; a common ratio is one **volume** quinoa to two volumes water. Simmer about **10–15 minutes**, rest **5 minutes**, check tenderness. Do not turn 150 g into 150 mL by assumption.
3. In the pot, soften onion in 10 mL oil for **6–8 minutes**. Add garlic, remaining spices and oregano for **30 seconds**. Add tomatoes, weighed chickpeas and 100 mL water; simmer **8 minutes**.
4. Add frozen brassicas and cook fully according to the packet, returning the pot to a simmer first. Add spinach for the final **1–2 minutes**. Add water only if sticking.
5. Fold in roasted vegetables, quinoa, lemon and pepper. Divide this base equally between five containers. Drain/weigh all sardines and divide by five separately, so every meal gets its share. Mash soft bones if preferred.
6. Cool promptly using the common procedure below. Add walnuts after reheating. If fewer than 600 g fish were recovered, record the actual grams; do not invent a 120 g portion.

## B. Lemon salmon, chickpea and green-vegetable rice bowls

**Five meals · about 50–65 minutes, plus cooling.** This is the lower-sodium, higher-cost salmon option. The cheaper canned version below is still nutritious.

### Exact ingredients

- **750 g raw plain salmon** in total, five 150 g portions. Example: Tassal Frozen Salmon Portions, 100% salmon; buy two 500 g packs and reserve 250 g for another meal. Keep unused portions frozen where possible.
- **150 g dry brown rice**.
- **400 g drained/rinsed no-added-salt chickpeas**.
- **500 g** plain frozen broccoli/cauliflower.
- **250 g** carrots, thinly sliced.
- **250 g** plain frozen peas.
- **150 g** kale leaves, finely chopped, tough stalks removed.
- **100 g** peeled onion, diced.
- **25 mL extra-virgin olive oil**: 10 mL for fish, 15 mL for vegetables.
- **10 g** garlic, **1 tsp oregano**, **¼ tsp pepper**, **40 mL lemon juice**, optional zest of half a lemon.
- **100–200 mL water**, as needed for vegetables.
- **50 g walnuts**, kept separate.

### Method

1. If thawing fish, thaw covered in the fridge on the bottom shelf, follow pack timing, and keep it separate from ready-to-eat ingredients. Do not thaw it on the bench. If individual frozen portions do not allow exactly 750 g without thawing extra, cook the surplus for another meal rather than refreezing thawed raw fish.
2. Heat oven to **180°C fan**. Cook brown rice to its packet, commonly **25–40 minutes**. If rice finishes first, cool promptly in a shallow dish; do not hold it warm on the bench while everything else cooks.
3. Pat thawed fish dry. Place on a lined tray, coat with 10 mL oil, oregano, pepper and optional zest. Bake **12–20 minutes** depending on thickness, until **63°C in the thickest part**. Time is only a guide. For cooking from frozen, follow the pack's longer method and verify the temperature.
4. Meanwhile soften onion and carrot in 15 mL oil for **8–10 minutes**. Add garlic for 30 seconds, then chickpeas and 100 mL water. Add frozen brassicas/peas and chopped kale; cover, return to a simmer and cook for the longest applicable packet time until hot and tender. Add water if needed.
5. Add lemon. Divide rice and vegetables separately into five equal portions. Add one cooked salmon portion to each. Raw 150 g portions will weigh less after cooking; do not label them 150 g cooked.
6. Cool promptly, then freeze as cooked meals. Add 10 g walnuts at serving.

**Budget version:** replace the raw fish with **500 g total drained pink salmon with edible bones**, 100 g per bowl, distributed after cooking the base. Buy enough cans to yield that drained amount; two 415 g tins are a practical allowance, but weigh. Skip the fish-baking step and use its 10 mL oil in the vegetables. This lowers the illustrative meal energy to roughly **550 kcal** and costs less, but increases sodium relative to plain salmon and changes calcium/fat content. It is not an inferior meal overall.

**Smaller appetite:** split a completed B portion into two smaller meals and add another tolerated food later if hungry. Alternatively use the canned option. Do not label two half portions with the full portion's nutrition.

## C. Ginger, pumpkin, red-lentil and tofu curry

**Five meals · about 50–60 minutes, plus cooling. No coconut cream required.**

### Exact ingredients

- **750 g Pureland High Protein Firm Tofu**, cubed.
- **175 g dry red split lentils**, inspected/rinsed.
- **150 g dry brown rice**.
- **400 g** no-added-salt diced tomatoes.
- **500 g** peeled/deseeded pumpkin, 2 cm cubes.
- **250 g** carrots, thin slices.
- **300 g** frozen broccoli/cauliflower.
- **150 g** spinach.
- **150 g** peeled onion.
- **25 mL extra-virgin olive oil**: 15 mL at the start, 10 mL at the end.
- **10 g** garlic, **30 g** grated fresh ginger.
- **2 tsp cumin**, **2 tsp coriander**, **1 tsp turmeric**, **¼ tsp pepper**; chilli optional.
- **40 mL lemon juice**.
- **700 mL water initially**, with another 200–400 mL available.
- **50 g walnuts**, separate.

### Method

1. Start brown rice using the packet's water/time. Set aside safely if it finishes early.
2. Soften onion and carrot in 15 mL oil over medium heat for **8 minutes**. Add ginger, garlic and spices; stir **30 seconds**.
3. Add lentils, pumpkin and 700 mL water. Bring to the boil, reduce to a simmer, partly cover and cook **15–25 minutes**, stirring frequently. Add small amounts of water if thick before pumpkin and lentils soften. Red split lentils should collapse; whole brown lentils are not a direct timing substitute.
4. Once lentils are soft, add tomatoes and tofu; simmer **8–10 minutes**. Add brassicas and cook fully for the packet time; add spinach at the end to wilt. The curry should be moist enough to spoon, not a dry paste.
5. Stir in remaining 10 mL oil and lemon. Taste before considering salt. Divide cooked rice by five and curry by five; combine one portion of each per meal. Cool and freeze. Add 10 g walnuts after reheating.

## D. Smoky black-bean, mushroom and tofu quinoa bowls

**Five meals · about 45–60 minutes, plus cooling.**

### Exact ingredients

- **750 g Pureland High Protein Firm Tofu**, drained/cubed.
- **500 g drained/rinsed black beans**, preferably no added salt.
- **150 g dry quinoa**.
- **400 g** no-added-salt diced tomatoes.
- **300 g** mushrooms, quartered.
- **300 g** red capsicum, deseeded/chopped.
- **300 g** zucchini, thick half-moons.
- **150 g** kale leaves, chopped.
- **150 g** peeled onion.
- **25 mL extra-virgin olive oil**.
- **10 g** garlic, **2 tsp cumin**, **2 tsp smoked paprika**, **1 tsp oregano**, **¼ tsp pepper**.
- **40 mL lemon juice**; optional chilli.
- **100–200 mL water** as needed.
- **50 g walnuts**, separate.

### Method

1. Rinse and cook quinoa according to its packet; rest 5 minutes after absorption.
2. Heat 15 mL oil in a wide pot. Soften onion for **5 minutes**, add mushrooms and cook **6–8 minutes**. Add capsicum/zucchini and cook **5 minutes**. Avoid scorching; a splash of water is fine.
3. Add garlic/spices/oregano for **30 seconds**. Add tomatoes, beans, tofu and 100 mL water. Simmer gently **10–12 minutes**, stirring carefully so tofu holds its shape.
4. Add chopped kale and simmer **4–6 minutes**, until tender and hot throughout. Add water if the sauce gets dry.
5. Stir in lemon and the remaining 10 mL oil. Divide quinoa and the tofu/bean mixture independently by five. Cool/freeze. Add 10 g walnuts after reheating.

## Fruit, calcium, iodine and other meals

Have one piece of whole fruit with or after lunch if it fits your appetite. Twenty oranges require twenty fruit, not a guessed bag count. Rotate with apples, pears, kiwifruit or seasonal fruit rather than treating oranges as uniquely optimal.

Optional: **250 mL So Good High Protein Almond** supplies 300 mg calcium and about 10 g protein on the inspected manufacturer label. Twenty drinks require **5 L**. It contains soy/almonds. A suitably labelled fortified soy milk is also useful; use its own nutrition panel. Shake and refrigerate after opening as directed. Fortified food can help a dairy-free diet; it is not unhealthy simply because it is processed.

Lunch does not need to cover an entire day's nutrition. Iodine, B12, vitamin D, total calcium, total energy and other nutrients still depend on the whole diet. Use iodised salt if adding salt, but do not add large amounts to chase iodine. No supplement or kelp powder is included. A handful of greens is not a verified full calcium strategy.

All four meals are rich in legumes/vegetables. If uncomfortable, use smaller portions and build up gradually, or reduce the legume amount and replace with tolerated tofu/grain. More fibre and more ingredients are not automatically improvements.

## Cooling and reheating: part of every recipe

- **Cook one five-portion batch at a time** unless fridge capacity allows more. Label portions with recipe, date and actual yield.
- Divide promptly into shallow dishes. An ice-water bath around a metal tray can speed cooling; stir with a clean utensil. Put small portions in the fridge promptly, **within two hours**, with air space around them. Do not leave a large pot on the bench to cool completely or stack hot containers.
- A useful thermometer benchmark is **60°C to 21°C within two hours, then to 5°C within another four hours**. This does not allow six hours on the bench. Fridge below 5°C; freeze cold meals preferably on cooking day at −18°C or colder.
- NSW consumer guidance: refrigerated leftovers up to **three days**, cooked rice/pasta up to **two days**, frozen leftovers up to **three months**. Thaw in the fridge and use within **24 hours**. For thawed raw fish follow any stricter label instructions. Cooking thawed fish into a new meal is distinct from refreezing it raw; only freeze the cooked meal if thawing, cooking and cooling have been safe.
- Carry in an insulated bag with an ice brick and refrigerate at work. Microwave until steaming throughout, stir halfway and allow standing time. Check the centre. A **75°C centre** is a conservative practical target; NSW's consumer page specifies at least 60°C/steaming hot. Microwave minutes vary with wattage and portion size. Reheat rice only once.
- The handling-time rule is separate from the controlled initial cooling process above. For later time out of refrigeration, cumulative time at 5–60°C: less than two hours, use or refrigerate; two–four hours, use immediately; over four hours, discard. Reheating cannot reliably rescue food mishandled beforehand.
