### Back-of-envelope per-portion nutrition, not laboratory results

The [September evidence review](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1nFJ4_F5GCUiHIVYu9zFZaqDrK1s6g0Tr/view) estimated an 18-portion lunch at **~330–370 kcal, ~18–20 g protein, ~10–12 g fibre**. These ranges remain uncertain because drained weights, onion, sauces and product labels were incomplete. It is a nutrient-dense but fairly small standalone lunch; a piece of whole fruit and a calcium-fortified soy drink can complement it.

Cooking method
Best workflow: start the root vegetables first, cook quinoa while they roast, then build the bean sauce. Total active
time is about 35-45 minutes; elapsed time about 60-75 minutes before cooling.
1
Heat oven to 200 C fan-forced. Line 2 large trays; use a third if your trays are small. Keeping vegetables
uncrowded matters more than squeezing them onto two trays.
2
Start the dense roast vegetables. Toss 722 g sweet potato + 286 g pumpkin + 508 g carrots with 20 mL
EVOO. Roast for 15 minutes.
3
Add the quick-roasting vegetables. Toss 287 g capsicum + 294 g zucchini with the remaining 10 mL roasting
EVOO. Roast everything for another 20-25 minutes, until tender and well browned at the edges.
4
Cook the quinoa. Rinse 300 g quinoa very well. Add 600 mL water, bring to the boil, cover and simmer on low
for 14-16 minutes. Turn off heat, rest 5 minutes, then fluff.
5
Prepare the nuts. Lightly toast 75 g cashews if you like. Keep the 75 g walnuts raw or only very lightly
warmed. Cool and refrigerate together in a sealed jar. Do not freeze them in the meals.
6
Make the soy-chickpea cream. Measure about 120 g drained chickpeas from the two tins and blend
completely smooth with 250 mL calcium-fortified unsweetened soy milk.
7
Build the flavour base. Cook 300 g diced brown onion in 10 mL EVOO over medium heat for 6-8 minutes. Add
cumin, coriander, turmeric, curry powder, garlic powder and black pepper; stir for 60 seconds.
8
Simmer the bean sauce. Add the remaining chickpeas plus the black beans, mixed beans and kidney beans.
Add 56 g fresh tomato, 150 g salsa, 1 stock cube and 350 mL water. Simmer uncovered 12-15 minutes. If
too thick, add water only in 25 mL splashes.
9
Add the greens and cream. Stir in the blended soy-chickpea cream, 173 g broccoli and 146 g frozen mixed
vegetables. Cook 4-5 minutes, keeping the broccoli slightly firm.
10 Finish off the heat. Fold in all roasted vegetables, cooked quinoa, 10 drained salmon tins, 136 g baby
spinach and 30 mL balsamic vinegar. Residual heat will wilt the spinach.
11 Season once, at the end. Taste first. Add more acidity or pepper if needed. Only then add up to 1/2 tsp iodised
salt across the whole batch. You may need less because salmon, salsa and stock already add sodium.
12 Divide into 10 equal containers. For maximum consistency, weigh the finished mixture, subtract the vessel
weight, and divide the food weight by 10. That is your target grams per container.

• Do not leave a huge pot warm for hours and do not
stack warm containers.
• Refrigerate promptly; freeze once cold.
• At work, reheat until steaming hot throughout.

Why this is close to the practical ceiling
Legumes: five tins across ten meals provide a substantial bean/chickpea serve and a large fibre contribution.
Fish: one small salmon tin per bowl gives regular oily fish without making fish the whole meal.
Whole-food carbohydrate: quinoa, sweet potato and legumes replace refined pasta/rice as the base.
Fat quality: EVOO, walnuts, cashews and salmon make the fat profile predominantly unsaturated; coconut milk and
processed meat are deliberately absent.
Plant diversity: quinoa, four legume types, soy, nine vegetables, walnuts, cashews and multiple herbs/spices.
Sodium: beans are rinsed, only one stock cube is used, and salt is added only after tasting.
Calcium note: 250 mL soy milk spread across ten bowls is not a full calcium serve per lunch. For a dairy-free diet,
include a reliable calcium-rich food elsewhere in the day - for example a full 250 mL serve of calcium-fortified soy
milk.
