02 / BORROW THE USEFUL PART

Ideas worth examining

These are different kinds of evidence. A person’s routine documents what they do; it does not isolate what caused their results.

Population observations + branded framework

Blue Zones

Use: plant-rich meals, beans as a regular staple, everyday movement and social connection. Make healthy defaults convenient.

Limit: observations of long-lived communities cannot prove that one ingredient, the “80% full” rule or the whole Power 9 package causes longer life. Genetics, healthcare and other circumstances also differ.

In this kitchen: choose a bean or lentil meal regularly and enjoy it with others when possible. Skip the wine-for-health message.

Bryan Johnson / Blueprint

Use: preparation, consistency and explicit routines. His published protocol includes diet, sleep, exercise and extensive monitoring.

Lessons from HealthiestPeople research

Consistency & useful measurement

The companion research separates public case studies, measured outcomes and self-report. Its useful themes include affordable routines, preserving function and measuring only what informs a decision.

Try: a brief weekly review of meals prepared, how the routine felt and one practical adjustment. Ask a clinician about appropriate risk-factor checks rather than chasing a “youngest biological age” ranking.

Limit: personal tracking and an epigenetic clock reading do not establish a treatment effect or a lifespan gain.

04 / A FLEXIBLE EATING RHYTHM

Meal timing without a magic clock

Start with nourishing food and enough of it. The following schedule is a convenience example, not a requirement, prescription to fast, or evidence that 7:30am or 6:00pm maximises human longevity. The dated planner lets you edit every time and add or remove snacks.

Example timeFoodPractical reason
7:30 amWholegrain oats, berries, walnuts and fortified soy; or eggs with vegetablesConvenient substantial breakfast; add enough to satisfy hunger
12:30 pmOne freezer lunch with beans/tofu/fish, grains, vegetablesPredictable midday meal that reduces takeaway reliance
6:00 pmVegetable-rich dinner with legumes, tofu, fish or poultryA comfortable family dinner, with time to digest before sleep
As neededFruit, nuts, a suitable fortified plant yoghurt or vegetables and a safe dipSnacks are optional; no evidence supports requiring two every day

Interval arithmetic: finishing food at 6:00pm and breakfast at 7:30am is about 13½ hours without food; a 7:30am–6:00pm eating window is 10½ hours. These figures describe a schedule, not an established optimal fasting duration. If you need an evening snack or later dinner to meet energy needs, eat one; children, pregnancy and medical circumstances need individual planning.

Evidence for eating earlier

A 2026 BMJ Medicine network meta-analysis included 41 randomised studies and 2,287 adults. Earlier time-restricted eating generally improved weight and fasting insulin more than late schedules, but the median study was only eight weeks and no lifespan outcome was measured. Direct timing comparisons remain limited; this cannot pick an exact best hour.

2026 trials meta-analysis ↗

Fasting is not required

In a 12-month randomised study of 139 adults with obesity, adding an 8am–4pm eating window to calorie restriction did not significantly improve weight loss beyond calorie restriction alone. This does not disprove every meal-timing effect, but it argues strongly against selling strict 16:8 fasting as a necessary longevity habit.

NEJM 2022 randomised trial ↗

Snacks: preference, not a quota

Australian meal-planning advice acknowledges that some adults prefer three meals without snacks, while others benefit from smaller meals with snacks. A systematic review of 16 trials found no clear cardiometabolic advantage to eating more frequently, with very-low-certainty evidence. Plan snacks for hunger, preferences and adequate energy rather than making the phone dictate extra meals.

Australian meal-planning guidance ↗ · Eating-frequency review ↗

Use real portions and adequate food

Even a healthy time-restricted schedule can backfire if it replaces sufficient protein, calcium, fibre or total energy with hunger. Consider maintaining regular meal times for convenience and protecting sleep, but don't skip dinner or add long fasts to increase a “longevity score”. There is no validated numerical score here.

Heart Foundation eating pattern ↗

Important: Children should not be put on adult time-restricted eating plans. Anyone pregnant, managing diabetes with glucose-lowering medicines, underweight, recovering from disordered eating or otherwise medically vulnerable should seek personalised advice rather than following an online fasting clock.

05 / HEALTHY ALSO MEANS ENOUGH

Check the gaps

Calcium & B12

A dairy-free or vegan pattern needs deliberate alternatives. Look for at least 100 mg calcium per 100 mL in a plant drink. Check tofu calcium in milligrams: the ingredient “calcium sulphate” is not a quantity.

Fully vegan diets need reliable B12 from fortified foods or supplementation. Do not treat unfortified fermented foods as a dependable B12 source.

Calcium guidance ↗ · B12 guidance ↗

Iodine without guesswork

Australian bread generally uses iodised salt, with exceptions including organic bread and home bread mixes. Check ingredients; dairy-free or bread-free eating can remove familiar sources.

Seaweed iodine varies widely. Kelp is not a predictable do-it-yourself iodine dose. Do not increase salt indiscriminately to meet iodine needs.

FSANZ: bread fortification ↗ · Seaweed findings ↗

Energy & protein

Restriction is not automatically healthier. Include protein foods, enough food to support activity, and strength training. These lunch recipes do not establish a full day’s energy or protein requirements.

Personal needs change with age, pregnancy, illness, training and body size. Use an Accredited Practising Dietitian for a complete nutrient assessment when needed.

NIA: restriction and its limitations ↗

Pattern before products

Vegetables, whole fruit, pulses, wholegrains and nuts provide variety; fish, eggs or suitable plant proteins can fit. Fortified foods can be useful even though they are processed.

No proprietary powder, organic label or supplement stack is required to follow this guide. Check specific deficiencies and indications before adding supplements.

06 / PROMISING DOES NOT MEAN PROVEN

A calmer look at gut health

Small human intervention study

Fermented foods

A 2021 study analysed 36 adults over 17 weeks. The fermented-food arm increased microbiome diversity and reduced several inflammatory markers. These are interesting intermediate outcomes, not evidence of longer life or a treatment for anxiety.

Practical option: try a small amount of a suitable fermented food you enjoy. Check sodium, added sugars and tolerance. Vinegar pickles are not automatically live-culture fermented foods; satay-flavoured tofu is not automatically fermented.

Wastyk et al., Cell (2021) ↗
Useful variety · no magic threshold

Fibre without a competition

Rotate legumes, grains, nuts, seeds, vegetables and fruit. Start with portions you tolerate and build gradually, with enough fluid.

There is no need to hit a “30 plants” badge to use this site. A count cannot tell you whether you ate enough food or met calcium, B12 or protein needs. Ongoing gut symptoms deserve assessment, not ever more fibre.

Human trial · intermediate outcomes

Gut biomarkers

Microbiome or inflammation changes are not the same as fewer illnesses or longer survival. Larger, longer studies would strengthen the case for specific fermented-food prescriptions.

Longevity outcome unestablished

Fasting & calorie restriction

Weight and metabolic changes can be studied without demonstrating longer human life. Do not copy a severe deficit or narrow eating window from an influencer. Adequacy, safety and sustainability come first.

Biological-age scores

Ask: was the outcome a validated clinical event, a biomarker, or self-report? Was there a comparison group? Were methods consistent? What else changed? A younger clock result is not a guaranteed extra year of life.
