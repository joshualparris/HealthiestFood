# Full-repository longevity and nutrition audit — 16 October 2026

**Status:** source-reviewed, recommendations only; no recipe has been demonstrated to extend lifespan. **Scope:** all recipe versions and historical archives available on `main` on 16 October 2026, including V2, V3, V4, V5, V6, the archived cook-up records, shopping/evidence documents and supporting calculators. Latest inspected tree included **32 files**. Earlier cook-ups are evidence of food preparation, **not** verified long-term consumption.

## Executive verdict

**The base food pattern is already very good.** Legumes, colourful vegetables, wholegrains, nuts, olive oil and fish or soy proteins are consistent with the Australian Heart Foundation's food-pattern guidance. The largest remaining opportunities are **(1) reliably meeting dairy-free daily calcium and other micronutrient needs; (2) whole-day/week diversity rather than maximal ingredients per lunch; (3) accurate, reproducible total nutrient calculations; (4) affordable meals you genuinely enjoy; and (5) rigorous safe cooling and reheating.**

V6 is the most operationally varied *one-supermarket* option (four five-meal recipes). V5 has the advantage of a **quantified-calcium Pureland tofu** and an explicit, albeit assumption-heavy, energy/protein model. V4 also used Pureland, but just two larger batch flavours. **No evidence establishes V6 as nutritionally or longevity-superior to V5.** V6 is simpler to shop from Woolworths; that is its documented advantage. Do not increase its subjective “health score” without real new evidence.

## Recommended improvements in order

### P0 — Meaningful changes to eating, health and safety

**1. Audit the _whole-day_ diet, especially dairy-free calcium (high priority).** V6's Evergreen Fresh Hard tofu is calcium-sulphate-set and reports **24 g protein/100 g**, but **does not report mg of calcium**. That is an *unknown quantity*, not a reason to say it has zero calcium. The alternative **Coles Pureland High Protein Firm 500 g** reports **17.9 g protein and 120 mg calcium/100 g**, so a 125 g serving supplies 22.4 g protein and 150 mg labelled calcium. This is measurably more certain, not proof the product is biologically superior. [Evergreen label](https://www.woolworths.com.au/shop/productdetails/595465) | [Pureland label](https://www.coles.com.au/product/pureland-high-protein-firm-tofu-500g-6848120).

For adults 19–50, the Australian **calcium RDI is 1,000 mg/day**, assessed across all meals. A consistent fortified soy milk could help. This is a whole-day target, **not** a requirement for a 1,000 mg lunch. [Australian Nutrient Reference Values](https://www.eatforhealth.gov.au/nutrient-reference-values/nutrients/calcium).

**2. Offer an _unsweetened_ fortified soy alternative instead of declaring one drink optimal.** V6's optional [So Good High Protein Soy 1 L](https://www.woolworths.com.au/shop/productdetails/6021686) lists **10 g protein, roughly 400 mg calcium/250 mL**, and **1.8 g total sugar/serve** with some cane sugar/maltodextrin. Woolworths also lists [Vitasoy Protein Plus Unsweetened Soy 1 L](https://www.woolworths.com.au/shop/productdetails/564472), made from **19% whole soybeans**, with **10.5 g protein and no added sugar**; [Vitasoy's manufacturer panel](https://soy.com.au/product/soy-milk-protein-plus/) reports **300 mg calcium per 250 mL**. This is a reasonable choice if you prefer fewer additives, not established to reduce mortality more than So Good. **Trade-off: 100 mg less labelled calcium per glass**, and actual product/stock/price must be checked at Dubbo Woolworths. Some Vitasoy *other* products contain barley/malt/gluten; never substitute names without checking.

**3. Make the fish target about the _actual week_, not a recipe's apparent fish count.** V6 A has eight 110 g sardine tins with ~600 g drained fish spread over five bowls. V6 B uses five **125 g raw** Ocean Chef coho salmon portions (625 g raw); these aren't automatically 125 g *cooked*. The Woolworths Ocean Chef box lists **four 125 g raw serves per 500 g pack** and **60 mg sodium, 24.4 g protein per 125 g raw**. [Product label](https://www.woolworths.com.au/shop/productdetails/6050076). The Heart Foundation recommends **2–3 weekly fish serves (generally 100 g cooked)** and approximately 250–500 mg/d marine omega-3 through fish intake; fish from dinners also counts. Fish frequency and edible/cooked weights both matter. Do not add more fish just to increase a score. [Heart Foundation omega-3 guidance](https://www.heartfoundation.org.au/healthy-living/healthy-eating/omega-3-omega-6-heart-health). Salmon/sardines are reasonable fish varieties; use official [FSANZ mercury guidance](https://www.foodstandards.gov.au/consumer/chemicals/mercury), particularly for pregnancy/children.

**4. Build in an actual 4-day work-lunch option.** The V6 schedule displays a 20-lunch **four-week, Monday–Friday** calendar, which is appropriate only for five weekday lunches/week. For a four-workday lunch routine, **20 portions last FIVE weeks**. The simple schedule is **Mon A, Wed B, Thu C, Fri D × five weeks**, or reorder to taste. That schedules **two fish lunches/week**, subject to the actual cooked fish portion size, while rotating all four recipes. Don't claim all 20 were eaten in four weeks without evidence.

**5. Trial the real recipe rather than optimising hypothetical numbers.** The newer versions all say **proposed, not cooked**. Make a **one-portion test** of A/B/C/D before five-portions-per-batch. Record taste, texture after freeze/reheat, comfortable fullness, food weight, ingredient substitutions, actual tin drained yields, cost from till receipt, and whether you voluntarily want to eat it again. Good adherence beats unrepeatable theoretical “superfood scores”.

**6. Upgrade chilling and reheating instructions with a centre-temperature check, not just a clock.** A home refrigerator may struggle to chill 5 kg of dense, freshly cooked rice/legumes/fish at once. Split into shallow, spaced containers, refrigerate promptly and avoid crowding/stacking. NSW Food Authority consumer guidance says reheat leftovers until steaming hot (at least 60°C); its general entertaining-at-home guidance suggests **at least 75°C**, so **use a probe to check ~75°C in the centre where practical** and stir microwaved meals. Treat product-specific fish thaw windows seriously. Cooked rice should only be reheated once. [NSW leftovers](https://www.foodauthority.nsw.gov.au/consumer/food-extra-care/leftovers) | [NSW entertaining at home](https://www.foodauthority.nsw.gov.au/consumer/food-safety-home/entertaining-home). This is a safety recommendation, **not a claim that 60°C contradicts the Food Authority's leftovers page**.

### P1 — Improvements with good rationale, but uncertain extra longevity benefit

**7. Rotate the same high-quality food groups rather than stuffing more into every bowl.** The latest recipes already combine chickpeas, lentils, black beans, broccoli, cauliflower, kale, spinach, carrots, capsicum, zucchini, pumpkin, onion, mushrooms, walnuts and wholegrains. Across *different weeks*, rotate a few available foods (cannellini/kidney beans instead of a portion of chickpeas, bok choy or cabbage instead of some kale, seasonal red/orange/green produce, different wholegrain choices such as buckwheat or verified-GF oats, and a variety of affordable whole fruit). Frozen plain vegetables are fine. Don't prescribe “30 plants” or an exact species count as a proven longevity threshold: the evidence for a universal magic number is not there.

**8. Check energy adequacy and digestibility.** V5's own model estimated A ~680, B ~810, C ~620 and D ~590 kcal **before fresh fruit and optional milk**; V6 uses less salmon and tofu with different nutrient profiles, making these V5 numbers **not transferable**. Too little lunch can lead to later hunger; too much fibre can cause bloating. Neither maximum fibre nor minimum calories is a proven longevity strategy. Adjust dry grain, olive oil, protein and/or total portion to appetite, activity, weight goals and tolerance, with more emphasis on sustainably nourishing eating.

**9. Avoid making 20 identical weekly nutrient exposures for months.** Four rotating lunches can make workday eating easier; but breakfast, dinners and weekends still need variety. Don't assume all iodine, vitamin D, B12, iron, zinc, selenium, potassium or calcium requirements are covered by one well-labelled freezer lunch. Use actual foods and (when appropriate) professional dietary assessment; don't add kelp/high-dose iodine, multiple supplements or excess iodised salt to chase a perfect meal score.

**10. Keep cost and accessibility honest.** V6's 35-line full first-shop estimate was checked arithmetically at **$256.43**, including optional 5 L soy and leftover pantry packs, about **$12.82 per planned lunch** as a basket allocation. It is **not a Dubbo receipt or actual ingredient consumption cost**. V4/V5 are not comparable by price without normalising fruit, drinks, unused pantry goods and amount/type of salmon. Check actual Dubbo store availability and substitutes. Optimise for real repeat cost and time, not an expensive first basket.

### P2 — Repository reliability (fixes before publishing hard nutrient rankings)

**11. Use the current Australian Food Composition Database (AFCD Release 3).** The [FSANZ database](https://www.foodstandards.gov.au/science-data/food-nutrient-databases/afcd) has **1,588 foods**, revised vitamin D and up to **58 core nutrients** per food. For each recipe, calculate *per actual serving*: energy, protein, fibre, total/saturated fat, sodium, calcium, iron, iodine (where known), B12, vitamin D, folate, potassium, magnesium and (when possible) EPA/DHA and ALA. Use package labels for branded fish/tofu/fortified drinks; AFCD for unpackaged foods; document source type and missing values. Keep units: **dry grains/lentils, edible drained canned foods, raw fish/tofu, cooked finished meal**, optional drink and fruit separately. Report a range or unknown when unavailable rather than pretending to have exact clinical numbers.

**12. Improve validation tooling.** V6's `verify.py` (present at the time of final review) has useful internal assertions but **hard-codes the 35 prices and recipe ingredient totals**. If `SHOPPING.md` or `RECIPES.md` change independently, its static assertions could still pass. Future implementation should parse real structured recipe/shopping data, derive the 35-line sum from the live shopping table, check every ingredient unit and pack count, detect recipe-versus-basket differences, verify internal relative Markdown links, and use a CI test. V5's `calculate.py` models **energy and protein only**, using some generic assumptions; it is not a complete nutrient calculation.

**13. Make V6 discoverable without hiding historical provenance.** The README **now** links V6 recipes, shopping, scientific evidence and script; the V6 `EVIDENCE.md` and `verify.py` files were added while this audit was in progress. Earlier missing links are **resolved**, so don't claim they remain outstanding. Preserve historical recipes, variants and cook-up chronology; they are useful evidence but **not** prospective dietary trials.

**14. Retire “/100 longevity” framing for decision-making.** All V1–V6 scores use subjective, overlapping 10-category rubrics; V5/V6/older ratings aren't measurements on a clinical scale and should not be ranked to conclude extra years of life. Keep separate **evidence level**, **nutrient adequacy**, **meal-prep safety**, **price/availability** and **actual enjoyment/adherence** assessments. No number is meaningful until tied to a validated endpoint.

## Specific low-effort changes for the next shop

- Prefer the **existing V6 four-flavour model** for variety and manageable prep, or V5's Pureland tofu if the Coles shop is convenient and a numeric tofu calcium label matters.
- Consider **Vitasoy Protein Plus Unsweetened Soy 1 L** as a sugar-free-added alternative to So Good High Protein Soy, **without** treating the swap as medically superior.
- Count fish **across the actual working week and other meals**, not tin gross weight.
- Adjust the roster to **four workdays/five weeks** when relevant.
- Keep walnuts, flax, citrus, vegetables, olive oil and low-salt legumes. Don't add powders, probiotics, supplements, a bigger pile of seeds, or premium organic ingredients without a documented nutritional gap.
- Test four single portions, measure cooked yield, update the real retailer basket and fill in nutrient estimates from AFCD before declaring a further “V7” improvement.
