# V2: practical, evidence-led freezer lunches for Dubbo

**Prepared 16 October 2026. Makes 20 adult lunches: two batches of ten. New recipe designs, not yet kitchen-tested.**

This updates the [October two-batch plan](LATEST_20_LUNCH_CONCEPT.md). The repo also received a [Woolworths-only optimisation](OPTIMISED_20_LUNCHES_2026-10-16.md) while V2 was being prepared; that alternative is preserved. Compared with that plan, V2 uses declared-calcium tofu, eight full 100 g fish portions instead of ten variably sized fish portions, a smaller lentil/pea load, red-lentil curry rather than whole-lentil bowls, and a Coles/Woolworths shopping option. These are practical trade-offs, not proof that V2 beats it on every nutrient. The older recipes remain as a historical record. See [shopping and prices](SHOPPING_V2_DUBBO.md) and [evidence and corrections](V2_EVIDENCE.md).

There is no scientifically established single healthiest meal, ingredient brand or 100/100 recipe. V2 improves measurable features: reliable portions of oily fish, explicit protein quantities, dairy-free calcium options, sodium choices, manageable quantities, accurate shopping arithmetic and safe cooling. It does not promise longer life from these particular recipes.

## The 20-meal plan

| Batch | Portions | Protein per portion, in addition to legumes/grain/vegetables |
|---|---:|---|
| A: lemon, smoked paprika and Mediterranean vegetables | 4 | 100 g drained sardines, including edible bones |
| A: same base | 4 | 100 g drained pink salmon, including edible bones |
| A: same base | 2 | 150 g plain firm tofu |
| B: ginger, pumpkin, red lentil and tofu curry | 10 | 120 g plain firm tofu, 30 g dry lentils and 50 g peas |

Each lunch also gets **10 g unsalted walnuts**, added at serving. Add a piece of whole fruit. If useful for the rest of your day's calcium intake, have **250 mL calcium-fortified plant milk** alongside. These are substantial adult lunches, not a prescription for every person's energy needs.

For five lunches a week, eat two fish lunches weekly over four weeks. For four lunches a week, 20 lunches last five weeks; this batch then supplies only 1.6 fish lunches/week, so include fish at another meal. Fish guidance is based on the whole week, not a tin count. If fish already appears regularly at dinner, you can retain the earlier six-fish-lunch split instead.

## Batch A: lemon, smoky vegetable, bean and quinoa bowls

**Makes exactly 10 portions. Allow about 75–90 minutes including preparation, plus cooling.** Use a large pot and two roasting trays; split between pots if necessary. Ingredient weights below are edible weights unless otherwise stated.

### Ingredients

| Ingredient | Amount to use |
|---|---:|
| Dry quinoa, any plain 100% quinoa | 300 g |
| No-added-salt chickpeas | 2 × 420 g cans, drained/rinsed |
| No-added-salt red kidney beans | 1 × 400–420 g can, drained/rinsed |
| Black beans, lowest sodium available | 1 × 400–420 g can, drained/rinsed |
| Plain diced tomatoes, no added salt | 2 × 400 g cans, including juice |
| Sweet potato | 400 g, skin scrubbed or peeled, 2 cm cubes |
| Carrots | 400 g, scrubbed, 1 cm slices |
| Red capsicum | 300 g, deseeded, chunks |
| Zucchini | 300 g, thick half-moons |
| Brown onion | 250 g, peeled, diced |
| Frozen plain broccoli and cauliflower | 1,000 g |
| Baby spinach | 250 g |
| Extra-virgin olive oil | 60 mL total, measured |
| Garlic | 20 g peeled, minced |
| Ground cumin | 4 level metric teaspoons (20 mL) |
| Smoked paprika | 4 level metric teaspoons (20 mL) |
| Dried oregano | 2 level metric teaspoons (10 mL) |
| Ground black pepper | ½ teaspoon, more to taste |
| Lemon juice | 80 mL; zest of one washed lemon optional |
| Water for sauce | Start with 200 mL, add only if necessary |
| Iodised salt | Optional; start with none, maximum ¼ teaspoon for this recipe if needed |
| Sardines | 400 g drained total, with edible bones |
| Pink salmon | 400 g drained total, with edible bones |
| Plain firm tofu | 300 g total |
| Walnuts, kept separate | 100 g total |

Four bean cans should give roughly 900–1,050 g drained beans. Weigh the actual amount; the can's net weight includes liquid. Canned kidney beans are already cooked: do not replace them directly with dry kidney beans.

### Method

1. **Prepare.** Heat the oven to **190°C fan-forced**. Put two shelves in place. Wash fresh produce and drain/rinse beans. Have ten shallow containers ready; check fridge and freezer space before cooking.
2. **Roast.** Toss sweet potato, carrot, capsicum, zucchini and half the onion with **30 mL oil**, half the cumin and half the paprika. Spread across two trays, leaving space around pieces. Roast **30–40 minutes**, turn and swap trays after 20 minutes; stop when tender and lightly browned, not blackened. Crowded trays take longer.
3. **Cook quinoa.** Rinse the 300 g in a fine sieve. Measure its volume, then use twice that volume of water, or follow its packet. Bring to the boil, cover and simmer about **10–15 minutes**, then rest covered **5 minutes**. Check tenderness and add a little hot water if needed. Do not assume 300 g equals 300 mL.
4. **Make sauce.** In the pot, soften the remaining onion in **15 mL oil** over medium heat for **6–8 minutes**. Add garlic and remaining cumin/paprika; stir for **30 seconds**. Add oregano, tomatoes, beans and 200 mL water. Simmer **10 minutes**.
5. **Cook frozen vegetables.** Add the broccoli/cauliflower and bring back to a simmer. Cook according to the bag, until hot throughout and just tender, usually **6–10 minutes** after returning to a simmer. Add spinach for the final **1–2 minutes** to wilt. Avoid leaving greens bubbling for another half hour.
6. **Finish base.** Fold in roasted vegetables and quinoa. Add **15 mL remaining oil**, lemon juice, optional zest and pepper. Taste before adding any salt. The fish will add sodium to its portions.
7. **Divide correctly.** Weigh the base without the pot and divide by **10**. If it is too heavy to weigh at once, weigh it in several bowls and add the weights. Portion evenly while still warm, then add **100 g drained sardines** to four containers, **100 g drained salmon** to four, and **150 g tofu** to two. Warm cubed tofu in a small pan with a splash of water for **5–8 minutes**, following pack directions, before adding. No extra oil is needed.
8. **Cool promptly** using the procedure below. Label A-sardine, A-salmon or A-tofu and the cooking date. Keep walnuts separate and add **10 g** after reheating.

**Fish purchase:** six 110 g sardine tins supply 450 g if each actually yields 75 g drained, leaving 50 g; check the label and weigh. Buy two 415 g salmon tins as a practical allowance, use 400 g drained total, and promptly refrigerate or freeze any surplus for another meal. Do not count all 830 g as drained fish. Mash the soft edible bones through the fish if preferred.

**Expected container size:** start with shallow 1 L containers. Actual yield depends on evaporation and drained weights; allow roughly 650–800 g per meal including protein. Use two smaller shallow containers per meal if one would be deep or overfilled. If you divide into more than ten lunches, each has less protein, vegetables and energy; relabel accordingly.

## Batch B: ginger, pumpkin, red lentil and tofu curry with brown rice

**Makes exactly 10 portions. Allow about 65–80 minutes, plus cooling.** This replaces the original uncertain 1 kg shelled-edamame purchase with readily specified tofu and peas. It is a cost/availability improvement, not evidence that peas are healthier than edamame.

### Ingredients

| Ingredient | Amount to use |
|---|---:|
| Plain dry red split lentils | 300 g, rinsed and checked for debris |
| Plain firm tofu | 1,200 g, drained, 2 cm cubes |
| Dry brown rice | 300 g |
| Frozen plain peas | 500 g |
| Plain diced tomatoes, no added salt | 2 × 400 g cans, with juice |
| Butternut pumpkin | 600 g peeled/deseeded, 2 cm cubes |
| Carrots | 400 g, scrubbed and sliced |
| Mushrooms | 400 g, quartered |
| Brown onion | 250 g peeled, diced |
| Frozen plain broccoli/cauliflower | 500 g |
| Kale leaves, finely chopped | 200 g, tough stalks removed |
| Extra-virgin olive oil | 60 mL total |
| Garlic | 20 g peeled, minced |
| Fresh ginger | 30 g, grated |
| Ground cumin | 4 level metric teaspoons |
| Ground coriander | 3 level metric teaspoons |
| Ground turmeric | 2 level metric teaspoons |
| Black pepper | ½ teaspoon |
| Chilli flakes | Optional, ¼–½ teaspoon |
| Lemon juice | 80 mL |
| Water for curry | Start with 1.2 L, plus 200–500 mL as needed |
| Iodised salt | Optional, maximum ¼ teaspoon if needed after tasting |
| Walnuts, separate | 100 g total |

### Method

1. **Start rice.** Rinse 300 g brown rice. Cook with the amount of water and time on the packet, commonly **25–40 minutes**. If finished before the curry, spread into a clean shallow dish and refrigerate promptly rather than leaving it warm on the bench.
2. **Build flavour.** Heat **30 mL oil** in a large heavy pot. Cook onion and carrots for **8 minutes**, stirring. Add mushrooms and cook **5 minutes**. Add garlic, ginger, cumin, coriander, turmeric, pepper and optional chilli; stir **30 seconds**, avoiding scorching.
3. **Cook lentils.** Add rinsed lentils, pumpkin and **1.2 L water**. Bring to the boil, reduce to a gentle simmer and partly cover. Cook **15–20 minutes**, stirring frequently to prevent sticking. Add water in 100 mL increments if thick before the lentils soften. Lentils should be collapsing and pumpkin tender; cook longer if needed.
4. **Add tomatoes and tofu.** Add tomatoes and tofu, return to a simmer and cook **8–10 minutes**. Adding acidic tomato after the lentils soften avoids unnecessarily slow softening. The finished curry should coat a spoon but remain moist enough to reheat.
5. **Add greens and peas.** Add frozen broccoli/cauliflower, peas and kale. Return to a simmer and cook for the longest applicable frozen-vegetable packet time, usually **6–10 minutes**, until fully hot and tender. Do not assume frozen vegetables need only thawing.
6. **Finish.** Stir in the remaining **30 mL oil** and **80 mL lemon juice**. Taste before adding optional salt. If too sharp, add a little water or more cooked pumpkin; no sugar is necessary. If too bland, increase ginger, cumin or lemon zest before reaching for stock powder.
7. **Portion.** Weigh cooked rice and divide by ten. Weigh curry separately and divide by ten. Put one tenth of each into every meal, then cool promptly. Add **10 g walnuts** per meal after reheating.

**Substitutions:** 200 g spinach or shredded bok choy can replace kale. For the edamame version, replace the 500 g peas with **500 g shelled edamame beans**, not 500 g pods, and follow the packet cooking instructions. Do not buy salted roasted edamame snacks for this recipe. Nutrition and price change with substitutions.

## Buying tofu and calcium companions

The priced preferred option is **Pureland High Protein Firm Tofu 500 g (Coles)**: three packs supply exactly 1,500 g for both batches, with a declared **17.9 g protein and 120 mg calcium per 100 g**. That gives about **27 g protein/180 mg calcium** in A's tofu topping and **21 g protein/144 mg calcium** in B's tofu alone. These are label-based calculations, not total bowl nutrition.

If unavailable, **Macro Firm Tofu 450 g (Woolworths)** is a good lower-cost protein alternative. Buy four packs; use 1,500 g and keep 300 g for another meal. Its current listing declares **14.8 g protein/100 g**, but no calcium amount. Do not infer a large calcium contribution from the word tofu. The listed firming agent is 511, not proof of calcium fortification. With this swap the tofu contributes about 22 g protein in A or 18 g in B.

A practical optional drink is **So Good High Protein Almond 1 L**, declared **300 mg calcium and 10.2 g protein per 250 mL**, dairy-free and gluten-free on the inspected Australian manufacturer page. It contains **almonds and soy**. Five cartons supply twenty 250 mL portions. Shake before pouring and refrigerate opened cartons as directed. High Protein **Soy** is also a valid choice, but use its own label; do not transfer the almond product's numbers to it. High Protein **Oat** contains gluten on the inspected listing.

Fortified milk is useful when it fills a daily gap, not compulsory with every lunch. Neither meal alone guarantees adequate daily calcium, iodine, vitamin D or B12. Fish and fortified foods contribute; the remainder of the diet still matters.

## Nutrition: what is established and what is not

- Each meal has vegetables, legumes, a measured wholegrain/pseudocereal portion, an explicit protein component and unsaturated fats. Batch A includes about 370 g non-legume vegetable ingredients per portion before cooking losses; B about 315 g, excluding peas. These are ingredient weights, not a formal cooked-serve calculation.
- Each fish bowl includes a measured **100 g drained fish**. Eight such bowls provide **800 g** across the batch. Plant omega-3 from walnuts is not equivalent to fish EPA/DHA.
- Protein from toppings alone is roughly **18–27 g**, depending on tofu brand or fish; lentils, beans and grains add more. Total calories, fibre, sodium and micronutrients have **not** been fully recalculated from verified labels. Do not reuse V1's 500–650 kcal claim as a verified V2 result.
- Do not equate maximum fibre with maximum health. If these portions cause bloating, try a smaller portion or reduce legumes modestly and replace with tolerated tofu/rice. Cutting grain automatically, as V1 suggested, is not a reliable fix. Build up legumes gradually.
- Frozen vegetables are a sensible choice. Organic, fresh-only, expensive powders and exotic berries are not necessary upgrades. Keep flavours you actually enjoy.
- These recipes use ingredients without intentional dairy or gluten, subject to labels. **They are not automatically allergen-certified.** The Fresh Harvest lentil listing has a milk/wheat/gluten cross-contact warning. If strict avoidance is required, choose an appropriately labelled alternative; that named lentil is a comparison listing, not a blanket allergy-safe recommendation.

## Cool, freeze, transport and reheat

Use NSW Food Authority guidance; the cooling step is part of the recipe.

1. Make one ten-meal batch at a time unless your fridge can safely cool both. Clear space first. Twenty large warm meals can overload a domestic fridge.
2. Divide promptly into shallow containers or shallow metal trays. An ice-water bath around the trays helps remove heat; stir with a clean utensil. Do not wait for a huge pot to reach room temperature.
3. Put small portions into the fridge promptly and **within two hours**. Allow airflow; do not stack warm containers. Cover/protect from contamination, avoiding a tightly packed tower that traps heat. A useful thermometer check is **60°C to 21°C within two hours, then to 5°C within another four hours**; this is a food-business cooling benchmark, not permission to leave meals on the bench for six hours.
4. Freeze the portions once cold, preferably on cooking day. Label recipe/protein/date. Keep the freezer at **−18°C or colder**. Aim to eat within **three months** for the NSW leftovers guidance.
5. Keep refrigerated rice meals no longer than **two days** and other leftovers no longer than **three days**; freezing most portions on day one makes this simple. Thaw in the fridge and use within **24 hours** after defrosting.
6. Transport in an insulated bag with an ice brick and refrigerate on arrival. Reheat until steaming hot throughout; a **75°C centre temperature** is a conservative practical target. Stir halfway, allow microwave standing time, and check the centre rather than just hot edges. Microwave time depends on portion size and wattage, so no fixed minute count is reliable. Reheat rice only once.
7. Reheating cannot rescue food stored unsafely. Time between 5°C and 60°C is cumulative: under two hours, refrigerate or use; two to four hours, use immediately; over four hours, discard.
