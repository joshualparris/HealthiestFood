Lemon–paprika sardine bowls

A bright, savoury bowl with oily fish, chickpeas, roasted vegetables, spinach and quinoa.

Ingredients for 5 servings

  • 8 tins John West springwater sardines, 110 g (75 g drained each)
  • 2 tins Woolworths no-added-salt chickpeas, 420 g
  • 150 g dry plain quinoa
  • 400 g diced tomatoes, no added salt
  • 400 g frozen broccoli/cauliflower
  • 250 g carrots
  • 200 g red capsicum
  • 200 g zucchini
  • 100 g peeled brown onion
  • 150 g baby spinach
  • 25 mL extra virgin olive oil
  • 10 g garlic
  • 40 mL fresh lemon juice
  • 2 tsp ground cumin
  • 2 tsp smoked paprika
  • 1 tsp dried oregano
  • 0.25 tsp black pepper
  • 50 g walnuts, added after reheating
  • 5 oranges, served fresh

Method

  1. Prepare your trays: Preheat oven to 190°C fan. Slice carrots to ~1 cm; cut capsicum and zucchini into ~2 cm pieces. Wash and measure everything. Keep raw fish ingredients separate from ready-to-eat fruit and nuts.
  2. Roast the colourful veg: Coat carrots, capsicum and zucchini with 15 mL EVOO, 1 tsp cumin and 1 tsp paprika. Spread on uncrowded trays. Roast 25–35 minutes, turning once, until tender. Roast time is a guide. Avoid burnt edges.
  3. Cook the quinoa: Rinse 150 g dry quinoa thoroughly in a fine sieve. Add approximately 420 mL water to a lidded saucepan (2:1 water-to-quinoa BY VOLUME, approximate). Bring to the boil, reduce to low, cover and simmer about 10–15 min until water is absorbed. Stand covered off the heat 5 min, then fluff. Use actual pack directions if different. Two parts water to one part quinoa refers to volume, NOT 150 g to 300 mL. The 420 mL water is an estimate for 150 g quinoa. Read the actual pack if different.
  4. Simmer the chickpea sauce: Soften 100 g onion in 10 mL oil for 6–8 min. Add 10 g garlic and remaining spices for ~30 seconds; add 400 g tomatoes, both drained/rinsed chickpea tins and ~100 mL water. Simmer 8–10 min. Taste before adding salt; the sardines provide sodium.
  5. Finish the vegetables: Cook 400 g frozen broccoli/cauliflower fully to packet directions. Fold in 150 g spinach, roasted veg, quinoa and 40 mL lemon juice. Frozen vegetables must be fully heated, not just thawed.
  6. Weigh, divide & add fish: Divide the cooked shared base into five equal weights. Drain all eight sardine tins; weigh combined edible fish and divide by five (~120 g if 600 g recovered). Add fish and edible soft bones to each bowl. A 110 g tin contains only about 75 g of edible sardines.
  7. Chill, freeze & serve later: Portion shallowly; refrigerate or freeze within two hours. Date labels. Thaw in fridge, reheat once until steaming hot and stir midway. Top with 10 g walnuts per serving, plus an orange. Never stack a large pile of hot containers; ensure fridge airflow.

Nutrition note: ~30 g protein from sardines + chickpeas + quinoa before vegetables and walnuts (partial label-based subtotal, not full nutrition).

Lemon coho salmon bowls

Tender coho salmon with brown rice, chickpeas, peas, kale and a fresh lemon finish.

Ingredients for 5 servings

  • 625 g raw Ocean Chef coho salmon (125 g raw per serving; buy whole 500 g packs as needed)
  • 2 tins Woolworths no-added-salt chickpeas, 420 g
  • 150 g dry brown rice
  • 500 g frozen broccoli/cauliflower
  • 250 g carrots
  • 250 g frozen baby peas
  • 150 g de-stemmed kale leaves
  • 100 g peeled brown onion
  • 25 mL extra virgin olive oil
  • 10 g garlic
  • 40 mL lemon juice
  • 1 tsp dried oregano
  • 0.25 tsp black pepper
  • 50 g walnuts, add after reheating
  • 5 green kiwifruit, fresh

Method

  1. Thaw safely: Separate only the five 125 g raw salmon portions you need. Thaw covered in the fridge below 4°C and consume within 24 hours after thawing, per pack. Keep the remaining 375 g frozen. Do not thaw an entire pack on the bench or refreeze thawed raw salmon.
  2. Cook the brown rice: Rinse and cook 150 g dry brown rice to its packet directions (commonly 30–40 min). If ready before the vegetables, spread in a shallow dish and chill promptly. Cooked rice needs special attention to rapid cooling.
  3. Bake the salmon: Heat oven to 180°C fan. Brush five salmon portions with 10 mL EVOO, oregano and pepper. Bake about 12–20 min depending on thickness; check ~63°C at the thickest centre with a thermometer. A raw 125 g portion will weigh less after cooking.
  4. Simmer the vegetables: Sauté onion and carrots in 15 mL EVOO about 8 min; add garlic for 30 sec. Add drained chickpeas, ~100 mL water, frozen brassicas and peas. Simmer per packet directions. Wilt 150 g kale. Bring frozen vegetables fully back to a simmer.
  5. Divide into five meals: Add 40 mL fresh lemon juice. Divide cooked rice and veg/bean mix evenly into five shallow containers. Add one cooked salmon portion to each. Keep all portions shallow so the centre can cool quickly.
  6. Chill, freeze & serve later: Chill and freeze promptly. Reheat a single serving thoroughly until steaming hot, stirring halfway. Add 10 g walnuts and one fresh kiwifruit at serving. Cooked rice must not be reheated more than once.

Nutrition note: 24.4 g labelled protein from 125 g raw salmon alone, before chickpeas, rice, vegetables and walnuts.

Golden pumpkin lentil curry

A comforting golden curry with pumpkin, red lentils, tomatoes, hard tofu and fragrant ginger.

Ingredients for 5 servings

  • 625 g Evergreen hard tofu, raw
  • 200 g dry red split lentils
  • 150 g dry brown rice
  • 400 g plain diced tomatoes
  • 500 g peeled/deseeded pumpkin
  • 250 g carrots
  • 300 g frozen broccoli/cauliflower
  • 150 g baby spinach
  • 150 g peeled brown onion
  • 25 mL EVOO
  • 10 g garlic
  • 30 g grated fresh ginger
  • 2 tsp cumin
  • 2 tsp ground coriander
  • 1 tsp turmeric
  • 0.25 tsp black pepper
  • 40 mL lemon juice
  • 700 mL water for lentil simmer; add extra only as needed
  • 50 g walnuts after reheating
  • 25 g ground flaxseed after reheating
  • 5 oranges, fresh

Method

  1. Start the brown rice: Cook 150 g brown rice to packet directions; spread in a shallow container and refrigerate promptly if waiting. Rinse 200 g red split lentils. Red split lentils cook down soft; whole green lentils need a different method.
  2. Build the aromatic base: Soften 150 g onion and 250 g sliced carrots in 15 mL EVOO over medium heat for ~8 min. Add 30 g ginger, 10 g garlic, cumin, coriander, turmeric and pepper; stir 30 seconds. Keep the spices aromatic without burning.
  3. Simmer lentils and pumpkin: Add rinsed lentils, 500 g cubed pumpkin and 700 mL water. Bring to a boil, then simmer partly covered for 15–25 min until lentils collapse and pumpkin is tender. Add 200–400 mL water if thick. Stir frequently to prevent lentils catching on the base.
  4. Add tofu and greens: Add tomatoes and cubed 625 g hard tofu; simmer 8–10 min. Cook frozen broccoli/cauliflower to pack timing; fold in spinach to wilt. Don't treat the tofu's calcium as quantified: the Woolworths listing has no calcium mg figure.
  5. Finish and portion: Stir through remaining 10 mL EVOO and 40 mL lemon juice. Divide cooked rice into five equal amounts and curry equally into five shallow bowls, spreading tofu evenly (~125 g raw equivalent each). Weigh finished cooked components instead of assuming cooked weight equals raw.
  6. Chill and serve safely: Refrigerate/freeze promptly. Reheat once to steaming hot throughout. Add 10 g walnuts, 5 g ground flaxseed and one orange per serving. Freeze walnuts/flax separately, not buried in the curry.

Nutrition note: Hard tofu lists 24 g protein per 100 g: about 30 g per raw 125 g serving, before lentils and rice.

Smoky black-bean tofu bowls

Protein-rich hard tofu and black beans simmered with mushrooms, capsicum and leafy kale.

Ingredients for 5 servings

  • 625 g Evergreen hard tofu, raw
  • 2 tins Woolworths black beans no added salt, 420 g
  • 150 g dry white quinoa
  • 400 g plain diced tomatoes
  • 300 g mushrooms
  • 300 g red capsicum
  • 300 g zucchini
  • 150 g de-stemmed kale
  • 150 g peeled onion
  • 25 mL extra virgin olive oil
  • 10 g garlic
  • 2 tsp cumin
  • 2 tsp smoked paprika
  • 1 tsp oregano
  • 0.25 tsp black pepper
  • 40 mL lemon juice
  • 50 g walnuts, add after reheating
  • 25 g ground flaxseed, add after reheating
  • 5 green kiwifruit

Method

  1. Cook the quinoa: Rinse 150 g quinoa, then combine with approximately 420 mL water (2 parts water to 1 part quinoa by volume). Bring to the boil, reduce to low, cover and simmer 10–15 min until absorbed; rest covered off heat 5 min, then fluff. Follow actual pack directions if different. This grain is naturally gluten-free but check packaging if strict avoidance matters.
  2. Sauté onion and mushrooms: Sauté 150 g onion in 15 mL EVOO for ~5 min. Add 300 g mushrooms and cook 6–8 min until their moisture mostly evaporates. Let mushrooms brown gently, not burn.
  3. Add capsicum, zucchini and spices: Add 300 g capsicum and 300 g zucchini for 5 min. Add garlic, cumin, paprika, oregano and pepper; stir ~30 seconds. No stock cubes or added sugar are needed.
  4. Simmer tofu and beans: Add 400 g tomatoes, two drained/rinsed black-bean tins, 625 g diced hard tofu and ~100 mL water; simmer gently 10–12 min, stirring carefully. No-added-salt beans reduce unnecessary sodium.
  5. Finish the kale and divide: Add 150 g prepared kale and simmer 4–6 min until tender; stir in lemon juice and remaining 10 mL EVOO. Divide quinoa and tofu/beans equally among five shallow containers. Weigh cooked portions for consistent sizes.
  6. Freeze, reheat and finish: Chill quickly; freeze. Reheat once to steaming hot. Add 10 g walnuts, 5 g ground flaxseed and a fresh kiwifruit to each meal. Batch size is five meals, not twenty, for safer home cooling.

Nutrition note: About 30 g tofu protein plus ~5.4 g black-bean protein and ~3.7 g quinoa protein per bowl, excluding vegetables.

Berry, chia & walnut overnight oats

A fridge-ready breakfast with wholegrain oats, berries, walnuts and calcium-fortified soy. Dairy-free with checked labels.

Ingredients for 1 servings

  • 50 g plain rolled oats (gluten-free certified if needed)
  • 180 mL calcium-fortified unsweetened soy milk
  • 10 g chia seeds
  • 80 g frozen mixed berries
  • 15 g unsalted walnuts
  • 0.25 tsp cinnamon

Method

  1. Stir the base: Mix oats, soy milk, chia and cinnamon in a clean covered jar.
  2. Chill: Refrigerate below 5°C overnight (at least 6 hours). Use within 1–2 days; don't leave unrefrigerated overnight.
  3. Top and eat: For most healthy adults, use fruit according to its label; for pregnant people or those with weakened immunity, use washed fresh berries or frozen berries cooked thoroughly then chilled. Do not assume thawing eliminates pathogens. Add walnuts just before serving.

Nutrition note: Wholegrain, fibre, plant protein and optional fortified calcium; no validated longevity score.

Spinach, mushroom & egg scramble

A savoury breakfast adapted from the older family breakfast plan, served with wholegrain toast.

Ingredients for 1 servings

  • 2 large eggs
  • 70 g mushrooms, sliced
  • 50 g baby spinach
  • 5 mL extra virgin olive oil
  • 1 slice wholegrain bread (check dairy/gluten labels)
  • 1 small tomato

Method

  1. Cook vegetables: Heat oil, cook sliced mushrooms 4–5 minutes; add spinach until wilted.
  2. Scramble eggs: Whisk eggs and stir over medium-low heat until set throughout; avoid serving runny eggs to higher-risk eaters.
  3. Serve: Serve hot with toast and sliced tomato.

Nutrition note: Eggs provide protein; vegetables and wholegrain improve variety. Dairy-free only after checking bread.

Berry, banana, spinach & soy smoothie

A convenient breakfast inspired by a historical family plan. Whole fruit is usually more filling; drink a smoothie slowly as a meal, not in addition to meals when you are not hungry.

Ingredients for 1 servings

  • 250 mL unsweetened calcium-fortified soy milk
  • 100 g frozen berries
  • 1 small banana
  • 30 g baby spinach
  • 15 g natural peanut butter (allergen)
  • 10 g chia seeds

Method

  1. Wash and measure: Wash spinach. Follow the frozen-fruit label. For pregnant or immunocompromised people, use thoroughly cooked then chilled frozen berries, or well-washed fresh berries; thawing alone does not kill germs. Measure soy and toppings.
  2. Blend: Blend all ingredients until smooth; use enough liquid for comfortable consistency.
  3. Drink promptly: Drink straight away or cover, refrigerate below 5°C and consume soon.

Nutrition note: A whole-food option with nuts/seeds and calcium when labelled fortified. Check peanut/soy allergies.

Avocado, tomato & chickpea toast

No-fuss savoury wholegrain toast with legumes and vegetables.

Ingredients for 1 servings

  • 2 slices wholegrain toast (dairy/gluten labels)
  • 80 g no-added-salt canned chickpeas, drained/rinsed
  • 60 g avocado flesh
  • 1 medium tomato
  • 5 mL lemon juice
  • 1 pinch black pepper

Method

  1. Toast bread: Toast bread to preferred crispness.
  2. Mash topping: Mash rinsed chickpeas with avocado and lemon; add black pepper.
  3. Assemble: Top toast with chickpea mix and fresh chopped tomato.

Nutrition note: Legumes and avocado supply fibre and healthy fats, but this breakfast may be relatively modest in protein. Optional side: 250 mL So Good High Protein Soy adds 10 g labelled protein and 400 mg calcium, plus vitamin B12 and vitamin D; this drink is NOT automatically included in recipe ingredients or the shopping list. Choose according to the rest of your day, not a universal protein target.

Tomato lentil & vegetable soup

A balanced, reheatable family dinner with protein-rich lentils, vegetables and brown rice.

Ingredients for 4 servings

  • 240 g dry red split lentils
  • 400 g no-added-salt diced tomatoes
  • 400 g carrots, cubed
  • 150 g brown onion
  • 200 g frozen spinach
  • 20 mL olive oil
  • 750 mL water, add more as needed
  • 160 g dry brown rice
  • 2 tsp ground cumin

Method

  1. Start rice: Rinse and cook brown rice to pack directions; cool promptly if storing.
  2. Make soup: Sauté onion/carrot in oil 5 minutes, add rinsed lentils, tomato, cumin and 750 mL water; simmer 20–25 minutes until lentils fully soft.
  3. Finish safely: Cook spinach through, divide soup and rice into four portions; serve hot. Cool leftovers promptly in shallow containers and reheat once.

Nutrition note: Adapted from a family dinner plan; quantities are newly specified suggestions, not a previously measured historical recipe.

Lemon chicken, sweet potato & green beans

One-tray family dinner inspired by older household chicken-and-vegetable meals.

Ingredients for 4 servings

  • 600 g boneless skinless chicken thigh (raw)
  • 650 g sweet potato, trimmed
  • 400 g green beans
  • 20 mL extra virgin olive oil
  • 1 lemon, juice
  • 2 tsp smoked paprika
  • 2 cloves garlic, minced

Method

  1. Preheat and prepare: Heat fan oven to 200°C. Chop sweet potato evenly, coat with 10 mL oil and paprika.
  2. Roast chicken: Place chicken and sweet potato on separate clean trays; cook chicken until thickest centre reaches 75°C. Avoid raw-chicken cross-contact.
  3. Finish vegetables: Steam green beans until tender; season with lemon, garlic and remaining olive oil; serve four equal portions.

Nutrition note: Protein, colourful vegetables and unsaturated fat. Raw-chicken handling and thorough cooking are essential.

Smoky bean & vegetable chilli

A hearty plant-based version of an older family chilli dinner with beans, spices and brown rice.

Ingredients for 4 servings

  • 2 tins no-added-salt kidney beans 420 g each, drained/rinsed
  • 1 tin no-added-salt black beans 420 g, drained/rinsed
  • 400 g diced tomatoes no added salt
  • 300 g red capsicum
  • 200 g mushrooms
  • 120 g onion
  • 160 g dry brown rice
  • 20 mL olive oil
  • 2 tsp ground cumin
  • 2 tsp smoked paprika

Method

  1. Cook rice: Cook dry brown rice as per packet, keep hot or cool it quickly.
  2. Prepare vegetables: Sauté onion, capsicum and mushrooms in olive oil until softened.
  3. Simmer chilli: Add rinsed fully cooked canned beans, tomatoes and spices; simmer 15 minutes until hot throughout. Divide with rice; chill leftover portions promptly.

Nutrition note: Fibre-rich beans and vegetables; substantial servings. Make smaller portions if fibre-heavy meals cause discomfort.

Ginger tofu & rainbow veggie stir-fry

A quick family stir-fry using a reduced-sodium flavouring and plenty of vegetables.

Ingredients for 4 servings

  • 600 g firm tofu, soy allergen
  • 500 g frozen broccoli/cauliflower
  • 250 g carrots
  • 300 g red capsicum
  • 200 g mushrooms
  • 240 g dry brown rice
  • 20 mL olive oil
  • 20 mL reduced-sodium gluten-free tamari (check label)
  • 15 g fresh ginger
  • 2 cloves garlic

Method

  1. Begin rice: Rinse and cook 240 g dry rice according to its packet.
  2. Brown tofu: Drain/cube tofu, pat dry and brown in half the oil on a hot non-stick pan.
  3. Cook vegetables: Stir-fry carrots, mushrooms and capsicum in remaining oil. Add frozen broccoli/cauliflower and cook to packet directions until hot throughout.
  4. Finish: Toss tofu, ginger, garlic and 20 mL tamari through hot vegetables. Serve with rice; store promptly.

Nutrition note: Tofu calcium depends on its actual label; low-salt tamari helps limit sodium but is not sodium free.

Salmon, quinoa & roast vegetables

Optional fish dinner inspired by historic meal plans; count it toward the whole week's fish meals.

Ingredients for 2 servings

  • 250 g raw salmon fillet
  • 90 g dry quinoa
  • 300 g broccoli
  • 200 g carrots
  • 15 mL extra virgin olive oil
  • 1 lemon
  • 1 tsp dried oregano

Method

  1. Roast vegetables: Heat fan oven to 190°C; roast carrots with oil about 20–25 minutes until tender.
  2. Cook quinoa: Rinse and drain 90 g dry quinoa; add approximately 250 mL water (standard 2:1 water-to-quinoa ratio by volume). Bring to the boil, cover and simmer gently 10–15 minutes until the water is absorbed, then stand covered off heat 5 minutes and fluff. If your actual pack differs, use its instructions.
  3. Bake salmon: Bake salmon at 180°C fan about 12–20 minutes depending on thickness, to 63°C at the centre or pack instructions; cook broccoli fully.
  4. Serve: Divide across two plates and squeeze lemon. Reheat any leftovers thoroughly only once.

Nutrition note: Oily fish supplies omega-3. Do not assume fish dinner must be added on top of two fish lunches every week.

Crunchy vegetables & hummus

A practical savoury snack from the historical rotation for people who can safely eat chilled hummus; see the higher-risk food-safety alternative in the method.

Ingredients for 1 servings

  • 80 g carrot sticks
  • 80 g cucumber slices
  • 50 g hummus (check sesame and dairy labels)

Method

  1. Wash and slice: Wash carrots and cucumber; slice into safe bite-sized sticks.
  2. Serve: Measure hummus and enjoy promptly, keeping leftovers chilled. For pregnant or immunocompromised people, replace chilled hummus/tahini with a freshly cooked legume spread or an alternative considered safe for them.

Nutrition note: Vegetables and legumes add fibre; check salt/sesame allergens. Store-bought hummus/tahini is a higher-risk ready-to-eat food for people with weakened immunity.

Hard-boiled egg & cherry tomatoes

A make-ahead protein snack from the older family snack ideas.

Ingredients for 1 servings

  • 1 large egg
  • 100 g cherry tomatoes

Method

  1. Boil egg: Put egg in simmering water and cook about 10 minutes until fully set.
  2. Cool promptly: Cool under cold running water, refrigerate below 5°C; don't leave cooked eggs at room temperature.
  3. Serve: Wash and serve tomato halves alongside the egg.

Nutrition note: A protein snack; allergy is egg. Halve/quarter cherry tomatoes for young children if sharing.

Berries, soy yoghurt & walnuts

A dairy-free yoghurt and fruit option rather than the dairy Greek-yoghurt entry in the older plans.

Ingredients for 1 servings

  • 120 g plain calcium-fortified soy yoghurt, if available
  • 80 g berries
  • 10 g walnut pieces

Method

  1. Check label: Choose an unsweetened dairy-free soy yoghurt with verified calcium fortification if possible; otherwise calcium remains unknown.
  2. Assemble: Use washed fresh berries, or follow frozen berry package directions. Higher-risk people should use thoroughly cooked/chilled frozen berries instead of just thawed fruit. Top with walnuts and serve cold.

Nutrition note: Product-specific calcium, protein, price and allergen claims are unverified; not suitable for nut/soy allergy.
