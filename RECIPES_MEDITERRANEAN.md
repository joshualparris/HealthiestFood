Evidence-checked, not hype-checked
A genuinely strong repeatable lunch pattern - high in vegetables, legumes, whole-food carbohydrate, oily fish, soy, 
nuts and unsaturated fat. No recipe can be proven to be the single “healthiest possible”, so this guide keeps the 
strong parts and corrects the claims that were too confident.

Fact-check verdict
Bottom line: this is an excellent lunch template. The evidence supports the overall dietary pattern much more strongly 
than any “perfect” brand or 9.8/10 score.
1. Fish arithmetic needed fixing
The original 3 sardine + 2 salmon split was only about 435 g of fish across two weeks. The John West 110 g tin 
provides about 75 g drained fish. Revised here to 4 sardine + 2 salmon lunches = about 510 g total fish, or ~255 
g/week: roughly 2.5 standard 100 g serves/week.
2. “Healthiest possible” is too strong
There is no validated scoring system that makes this recipe 9.8/10 or proves it is uniquely optimal. Energy needs, 
appetite, the rest of the diet, variety across weeks and individual medical needs all matter.
3. Quinoa brand does not meaningfully decide longevity
Wellness Road Australian White Quinoa is a clean, practical choice: 100% quinoa and high fibre on its current label.
But the label showing more fibre than an organic quinoa does not prove the non-organic product is biologically 
healthier.
4. The salmon choice is good - for the right reasons
Coles Pink Salmon 210 g is 99% salmon, contains edible bones and lists 141 mg calcium plus 1.14 g omega-3 per 
100 g. Those are useful features. Calling it “substantially better” solely because another tin has a lower salmon 
percentage is too simplistic, because packing liquid changes percentages.
5. Fortified soy is useful, not mandatory
Vitasoy Protein Plus provides 300 mg calcium and 10.5 g protein per 250 mL, and meets the Australian Dietary 
Guidelines threshold for a calcium-fortified milk alternative. If your total daily calcium is already adequate, it does not
need to be forced into lunch.
6. Exact products can change
Online prices and availability are live retail information. The kidney-bean product in the original list has already 
shifted: the organic version is currently unavailable online, so this guide uses the readily listed Edgell Red Kidney 
Beans No Added Salt 400 g instead.

The recipe
Mediterranean sardine, salmon & tofu legume-quinoa bowls
Makes 10 deliberately large freezer lunches.
Shared base - use exactly
• 300 g dry quinoa • 1,000 g frozen broccoli/cauliflower
• 2 x 400 g chickpeas, drained and rinsed • 240 g baby spinach
• 1 x 400 g black beans, drained and rinsed • 60 mL extra virgin olive oil total
• 1 x 400 g red kidney beans, drained and rinsed • 4-6 cloves garlic, minced
• 2 x 400 g Ardmona diced tomatoes • 2 tbsp ground cumin
• 400 g peeled sweet potato, 2 cm cubes • 2 tbsp smoked paprika
• 400 g carrots, thick half-moons • 2 tsp dried oregano
• 300 g red capsicum, chunky pieces • 1 tsp black pepper
• 300 g zucchini, thick half-moons • juice of 2 lemons
• 250 g brown onion, divided • 0 to 1/2 tsp iodised salt, only after tasting
Protein split - corrected
4
SARDINE
1 drained John West tin in each bowl
2
SALMON
half the 210 g Coles tin in each bowl
4
TOFU
125 g roasted tofu in each bowl
Why 4 + 2 + 4?
This revised split uses the full tofu pack and gives about 510 g fish across two work weeks - roughly 255 g/week, 
close to 2.5 x 100 g fish serves per week.
Cook the batch
1
Roast the vegetables
Heat oven to 200°C fan-forced. Toss sweet potato, carrots, capsicum, zucchini and about half the onion 
with 35 mL EVOO, 1 tbsp cumin, 1 tbsp smoked paprika and black pepper. Spread over two trays and roast
30-35 minutes, turning once, until tender with browned edges.
2
Roast the tofu separately
Cut 500 g tofu into 2 cm cubes. Toss with 10 mL EVOO, a little smoked paprika and pepper. Roast 20-25 
minutes. Keep salty bottled marinades out of this base recipe.
3
Cook 300 g quinoa
Rinse thoroughly. Cook according to the packet; the current Wellness Road instructions use roughly a 1:2 
quinoa-to-water ratio. Once absorbed and tender, fluff with a fork.
4
Build the bean-tomato sauce
In your largest pot, soften the remaining onion and garlic with 15 mL EVOO. Add the remaining 1 tbsp 
cumin, 1 tbsp smoked paprika, 2 tsp oregano and pepper. Stir briefly, then add both tins of tomatoes, all 
four drained/rinsed bean cans and about 300 mL water. Simmer gently for 10-15 minutes.
Keep the flavour, lose the salt load
No stock cube, coconut milk, bottled pasta sauce or added sugar is needed here. Lemon, garlic, spices, roasting 
and EVOO do most of the flavour work.
Finish + portion
5
Add the cruciferous vegetables
Add the full 1 kg frozen broccoli/cauliflower to the sauce. Cook just until tender - around 5-7 minutes once 
everything is hot again. Longer cooking is not dangerous, but it can reduce texture and some heat-sensitive
nutrients.
6
Finish the shared base
Turn off the heat. Fold through all roasted vegetables, cooked quinoa, 240 g spinach and the juice of 2 
lemons. Taste. Add iodised salt only if needed, up to 1/2 tsp for the entire batch.
7
Weigh, then divide by 10
Put an empty large bowl on the scales and tare it. Transfer in the finished shared base and record the total 
weight. Divide that number by 10, then weigh that amount into each container. Example: 5,000 g base = 
500 g base per container. Do not portion by eye if you want ten equal lunches.
8
Add protein, chill and freeze
Four containers get one drained sardine tin each. Two get half the salmon tin each (about 105 g). Four get 
exactly 125 g roasted tofu each. Cool promptly in shallow containers, refrigerate, then freeze. Reheat until 
steaming hot throughout.
These are big lunches
The batch contains a lot of legumes, quinoa and vegetables. If this quantity routinely leaves you uncomfortably full, 
the healthier move is to reduce the portion or quinoa next batch - not to force a fixed “optimal” amount.

Why this is a strong long-term pattern
• Vegetables are the largest component, with leafy greens plus cruciferous, orange, red and allium vegetables.
• Legumes appear in every meal; canned no-added-salt options keep preparation realistic while limiting sodium.
• The fish frequency now genuinely matches the spirit of Australian 2-3 serves/week guidance much better.
• Sardines and salmon provide marine omega-3; edible fish bones and fortified soy contribute calcium.
• Tofu, beans and quinoa make the non-fish lunches protein-rich without processed meat.
• EVOO and walnuts provide unsaturated fats; the recipe avoids butter, coconut fat and creamy sauces.
• Whole fruit is used rather than juice; there is no added sugar in the recipe itself.
• The key health advantage is the pattern - not the brand names.
Do not over-optimise these
You do not need: organic quinoa, “superfood” quinoa, MCT oil, coconut oil, collagen, greens powders, goji berries, 
turmeric supplements, Himalayan salt or boutique “high-polyphenol” oils to make this lunch evidence-based.
The more meaningful next-batch improvement is variety: rotate legumes, grains/pseudocereals, leafy greens, cruciferous vegetables and oilyfish species over months.
