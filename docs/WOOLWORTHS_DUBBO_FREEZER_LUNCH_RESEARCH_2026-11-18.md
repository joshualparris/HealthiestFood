# Woolworths Dubbo: evidence review and 10 freezer lunches (18 November 2026)

> **Status: research-backed shopping and cooking proposal; NOT an individualised dietetic prescription or a claim of the world's healthiest meal.** Woolworths catalogue pages were checked 18 Nov 2026. Dubbo shelf stock, local checkout prices, exact drained yields, in-kitchen cooking temperatures and finished-meal laboratory nutrient values are **not verified**. This is editorial research, not a deployed app feature.

## Executive decision

Make **5 salmon + red lentil + quinoa + vegetable bowls** and **5 tofu + chickpea + brown rice + vegetable curry bowls**. Each is dairy-free **by recipe ingredients**; inspect each purchased packet's ingredients and allergen statements (red lentils listed below have a **may contain milk** precaution). Avoid treating the old informal 96/100 score as a scientific measurement. A varied, sustainable pattern is better supported than optimizing arbitrary ingredient counts or seed loads.

Evidence: [Australian Dietary Guidelines](https://www.eatforhealth.gov.au/guidelines/australian-dietary-guidelines-1-5); [Heart Foundation healthy pattern](https://www.heartfoundation.org.au/healthy-living/healthy-eating/heart-healthy-eating-pattern); [Heart Foundation fish and omega-3](https://www.heartfoundation.org.au/healthy-living/healthy-eating/omega-3-omega-6-heart-health); [FSANZ AFCD Release 3](https://www.foodstandards.gov.au/science-data/food-nutrient-databases/afcd/about-afcd).

## B. Exact batch recipes

### Recipe A — salmon, lentil and quinoa vegetable bowls (5 containers)

- Canned pink salmon **2 × 415 g tins** (brand's edible yield 4 × 83 g each = 664 g total); retain/mash soft edible bones if palatable
- Red split lentils **150 g dry**
- Quinoa **150 g dry**
- Frozen broccoli **400 g**
- Frozen spinach **200 g**
- Carrot **250 g**
- Onion **150 g**
- Red capsicum **200 g**
- Plain passata **400 g**
- Extra-virgin olive oil **20 mL**
- Garlic **10 g**, lemon juice **30 mL**
- Ground cumin **1 tsp**, paprika **2 tsp**, pepper to taste
- Additional potable water to cook grains and maintain saucy texture (follow packet instructions and evaporation; *not* added to final nutrition totals)

**Procedure:** (1) Rinse quinoa; cook 150 g dry according to its packet until tender. (2) Rinse lentils; simmer 150 g dry in ample fresh water around 10–15 min until cooked, drain any excess. (3) Heat 20 mL oil, soften onion/carrot 6–10 min, add capsicum/garlic/spices 2 min. (4) Add 400 g passata, broccoli and spinach; simmer until vegetables are cooked and the sauce is thick. (5) Gently fold in cooked grains/lentils and drained salmon, mash edible soft bones, heat thoroughly, turn off heat and add lemon. (6) Divide **by measured finished batch weight / 5**, not by assumed drained yield. Aim for ~600–750 g per container; actual weight will depend on moisture. Chill/freeze safely as below.

**Screening nutrition per lunch, approximate:** **~500–600 kcal (2,100–2,500 kJ); ~38–46 g protein; ~45–60 g carbohydrate; ~12–17 g fibre; ~12–18 g total fat; ~3–4 g saturated fat; ~500–750 mg sodium**. The **two salmon cans are the dominant sodium source**. These are *rough source-informed projections*, not AFCD3-cooked recipe outputs or measured lab totals; actual drained yield, products, cooking losses and brand labels matter. **EPA + DHA: present, exact mg UNKNOWN**, because the chosen salmon label does not report them. Calcium is potentially higher if soft salmon bones are eaten, but mg is **unverified**.

### Recipe B — tofu, chickpea, lentil and brown-rice vegetable curry (5 containers)

- Macro Firm Tofu **450 g**, diced (or confirmed calcium-set firm tofu)
- No-added-salt chickpeas **1 × 420 g can**, drained (~252 g as stated by serving scheme)
- Red split lentils **150 g dry**
- Brown rice **200 g dry**
- Frozen broccoli **300 g**, spinach **200 g**
- Carrots **200 g**, onion **200 g**, peeled pumpkin **400 g**
- Plain passata **300 g**, extra-virgin olive oil **20 mL**
- Garlic **10 g**, lemon juice **30 mL**
- Curry powder **2 tsp**, ground cumin **1 tsp**, turmeric **1 tsp**, black pepper
- Water **300–500 mL** while simmering, plus rice-cooking water per packet

**Procedure:** (1) Cook brown rice separately following packet; don't leave cooked rice warm on the bench. (2) Heat oil; cook onion/carrot 6–10 min. Stir in garlic and spices, then pumpkin, rinsed lentils, passata and ~300–500 mL water. (3) Simmer ~20–30 min, stirring and adding water if needed, until pumpkin and lentils are tender. (4) Add chickpeas, tofu, broccoli, spinach; simmer ~8–10 min until heated/cooked throughout. (5) Add lemon. Place cooked rice into **five** containers and cover with even curry quantities by scale. Aim for ~600–750 g each, actual weighed batch decides.

**Screening nutrition per lunch, approximate:** **~480–600 kcal (2,000–2,500 kJ); ~27–35 g protein; ~65–80 g carbohydrate; ~15–22 g fibre; ~8–13 g total fat; usually <3 g saturated fat; sodium likely much lower than salmon batch before added condiments**. This model uses the Macro tofu label (14.8 g protein/100 g; 6.2 g fibre/100 g), Woolworths chickpea label, red lentil label and generic food proxies. **Iodine, calcium, vitamin D, iron bioavailability, EPA/DHA, zinc and selenium are NOT quantified or assured**.

For one reproducible screening calculation, take per-100-g **pack labels** for salmon (581 kJ, 20.7 g protein, 382 mg sodium), tofu (541 kJ, 14.8 g protein, 6.2 g fibre, <5 mg sodium), quinoa (1,580 kJ, 12.2 g protein, 10.5 g fibre), chickpeas (267 kJ, 4.5 g protein, 5.9 g fibre), lentils (1,239 kJ, 24.2 g protein, fibre unlisted). Use approximate generic produce/raw grain composition where pack labels are unavailable. Approximate point-screening before rounding: **A ~535 kcal / 44 g protein / 14 g fibre / 620 mg sodium; B ~529 kcal / 31 g protein / 20 g fibre / 115 mg sodium**. These numbers **are not label-verified final cooked-container nutrients**. Where canned/vegetable brand values and drained weight differ, *do not quote exact decimals as fact*. ## C. Alternatives compared (no pseudo-clinical numeric health ratings)

| Contender | Strengths | Main limitations | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Improved salmon + lentil + quinoa + greens | Marine omega-3 source, good protein, plant diversity, substantial fibre | Salted canned fish can push sodium up; cost/taste; salmon EPA/DHA unquantified | **Use 5** |
| Sardines + wholegrain + vegetables | Marine omega-3, edible bones often give useful calcium, typically affordable | Fish flavour/texture, canned sodium; verify products and labels; freezing may change texture | Good **future** substitute for some fish bowls |
| Calcium-set tofu + chickpea + greens + brown rice | Plant protein, fibre, low saturated fat; can contribute calcium *if actually calcium-set* | No EPA/DHA; calcium depends on coagulant; high fibre may need acclimatisation | **Use 5** |
| Skinless chicken + lentils + roasted vegetables + brown rice | High-quality protein, broadly acceptable and freezer-friendly | No marine omega-3; chicken-only rotation less fish diversity | Valid alternative if fish unavailable |
| Mediterranean beans + wholegrains + olive oil + greens | Low-cost, high fibre, strong guideline alignment | May need extra protein/calcium planning; no marine EPA/DHA | Good economical substitute or third rotation |

**Variety rationale:** A two-recipe 5+5 rotation offers fish and plant proteins and different grain/vegetable patterns, with less repetitive eating. There is **no trial proving that this exact 5+5 combination is clinically healthier than any well-designed single recipe**, and no scientifically defensible 'absolute healthiest' food.

## D. Historical 96/100 recipe: what does and does not follow

The earlier 10-portion recipe included only **one 415 g can of salmon for ten meals**; its nutrition panel indicates **332 g labelled edible servings total**, i.e. ~33 g per container if spread evenly. This is considerably less than a normal canned-fish serve. It **cannot** establish adequate weekly marine omega-3 by itself. The old **150 g hemp seeds / ten meals = 15 g per meal**, **50 g flax / ten = 5 g per meal**: not inherently excessive, but extra omega-6/ALA/fibre/calories do **not** necessarily improve the recipe, and ALA is not equivalent to EPA/DHA. The **7 eggs / ten = 0.7 egg per bowl** are not intrinsically harmful, but are optional because the fish/tofu/legumes already deliver substantial protein; no demonstrated optimal egg number. Corn, carrot, greens, lentils and quinoa provide important variety but **do not guarantee complete calcium, iodine, vitamin D or B12 coverage across a day**. Nutritional yeast is not a reliable B12 source unless product *specifically fortified* and labelled. An arbitrary **96/100** ranking has **no medical or scientific calibration**, so never present it as 96% 'perfect nutrition'.

**Omega-3 target:** Heart Foundation encourages **2–3 fish/seafood serves weekly**, including oily fish, corresponding on average to ~250–500 mg/day marine EPA/DHA. It distinguishes marine EPA/DHA from plant ALA. Actual canned fish drained portions, weeks and other fish meals must be counted; don't falsely convert can gross weights to edible portions. [Heart Foundation](https://www.heartfoundation.org.au/healthy-living/healthy-eating/omega-3-omega-6-heart-health).

**Dairy-free adequacy:** For adult men 31–50, calcium RDI is **1,000 mg/day**; iodine RDI **150 micrograms/day**. Neither batch by itself guarantees whole-day adequacy. Look for appropriately fortified calcium-containing dairy alternatives across other meals, or calcium-set tofu with actual calcium labelling; seafood/fish/eggs/iodised salt can contribute iodine but exact figures cannot be assumed. [Calcium NRV](https://www.eatforhealth.gov.au/nutrient-reference-values/nutrients/calcium), [Iodine NRV](https://www.eatforhealth.gov.au/nutrient-reference-values/nutrients/iodine). Vitamin D and all other nutrients must be assessed in the broader daily eating pattern.

**Evidence hierarchy:** Dietary guidelines and the [Heart Foundation](https://www.heartfoundation.org.au/healthy-living/healthy-eating/heart-healthy-eating-pattern) offer actionable pattern guidance; [2025 umbrella review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11931306/) and [2023 prospective cohort meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36811594/) associate food groups/wholegrains with cardiovascular and mortality outcomes, but observational associations **do not establish a causal effect or lifespan gain from one lunch**. Replacement analyses [2023 meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37968628/) support swapping some animal foods for plant foods within overall dietary patterns, not universal elimination of fish. Definitive trial outcomes for individual seed brands, spice doses, exact vegetable counts, or these two recipes do not exist.

## E. Food safety (especially rice, fish and large 10-meal batches)

1. Arrange **ten clean, shallow, freezer-safe containers**; ideally use two cooking sessions or space in the refrigerator so hot containers do **not** heat up already chilled food.
2. After cooking, **divide promptly into shallow portions**, vent steam briefly, and refrigerate promptly. **Do not leave cooked rice/fish/lentils to cool completely on a counter for hours**. NSW consumer guidance advises chilling/freezing leftovers **within 2 hours**. Commercial two-stage benchmark: from **60°C to 21°C within 2 h, then to 5°C within the next 4 h**. These are *distinct* from the 2 h/4 h rules for food held out of refrigeration.
3. Once thoroughly cold, seal, label (recipe + date + allergen), and freeze around **−18°C**. For best texture, aim to eat in roughly **2–3 months**; quality guidance, not a claim food automatically becomes unsafe then.
4. Keep frozen or chilled under 5°C during transport (insulated bag + ice pack if needed). Thaw in the fridge when convenient; do not thaw on the kitchen bench.
5. Microwave until **steaming hot throughout**, stir thoroughly and check cold spots. Eat promptly; avoid repeated cooling/reheating.
6. No food-safety assurance is possible without observing actual home cooling, internal temperature and appliances.

Primary sources: [NSW Food Authority leftovers](https://www.foodauthority.nsw.gov.au/consumer/food-extra-care/leftovers), [NSW cooling requirements](https://www.foodauthority.nsw.gov.au/retail/cafes-restaurants-and-retail-outlets/cooling-food-safely-advice-food-businesses), [FSANZ cooling/reheating](https://www.foodstandards.gov.au/business/food-safety/cooling-and-reheating-food).
