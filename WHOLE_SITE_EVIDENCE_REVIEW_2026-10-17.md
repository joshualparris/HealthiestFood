# Whole-site, full-day health and longevity evidence review — 17 October 2026

**Review type:** public-source nutrition/food-safety review plus inspection of the HealthiestFood source, tests, recipes, calendar, V6 basket and longevity guide on `main`. **Not a medical review, personal dietary assessment, verified meal cook-up, Dubbo store checkout or live-browser acceptance test.** Neither exact recipe can be established to maximise lifespan. This document records *meaningful changes made* and *highest-priority next improvements*; no new arbitrary /100 longevity ratings.

## Main conclusion

The site's base Mediterranean-style pattern is already strong. The main difference between "a recipe with many healthy ingredients" and "a sustainable, nutritious diet" is **adequacy across the entire day/week, portion satisfaction, food safety, realistic expense, and actual adherence**. Focus next effort on labelled calcium/iodine and protein/fibre coverage, real food quantities, reducing habitual processed meats/refined grains, freezer reheating and not accidentally prescribing fasts or snacks. The corrected [PREDIMED](https://www.nejm.org/doi/10.1056/NEJMoa1800389) trial and [2023 Nutrition Reviews network meta-analysis](https://academic.oup.com/nutritionreviews/article/82/6/715/7222719) support Mediterranean dietary *patterns* for cardiovascular outcomes; neither tested these website meals, and individual longevity gains cannot be inferred.

## 1. Highest-confidence nutrition: specific Australian daily / weekly targets

These are *population guidance for healthy adults*, not automatic personal targets, minimums for every meal or substitute for a complete dietary assessment.

| Food/nutrient | Reference recommendation | Food-first implementation in this app | Major limitations |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vegetables and legumes | **Men 19–50: 6 Australian serves/day; women 19–50: 5/day**. One serve ≈**75 g** cooked vegetables or legumes, but leafy-salad and starchy equivalents differ. | Make vegetables part of both lunch and dinner; change colour/type through the week; one meal's grams alone don't certify all daily serves. | Other ages/stages vary; counts from raw freezer weights can be misleading. |
| Fruit | Usually **2 adult serves/day**. | Whole fruit with breakfast/another meal; count the fruit sides already included with V6 bowls before adding extra fruit. | The fruit in a smoothie is not automatically a superior replacement for whole fruit; serving size matters. |
| Wholegrains | Prefer mostly wholegrains. | Oats at breakfast, quinoa/brown rice with lunch, and optionally grainy bread/other wholegrains at dinner. | Gluten-free alone does not mean high-fibre wholegrain; rice and quinoa need correct dry-vs-cooked conversion. |
| Dietary fibre | **Adults: 30 g/day men, 25 g/day women (adequate intake)**. | Lentils, beans, oats, fruit, greens; increase gradually with adequate fluids if current intake is low. | More isn't always better for GI comfort; **the app does not yet have validated per-day fibre totals**. |
| Calcium | **1,000 mg/day** for most adults age 19–50; higher for some older groups. | Fortified soy drink **check calcium in mg/100mL**; canned fish with edible bones where relevant; numeric calcium-labelled tofu. | Evergreen calcium-sulphate-set tofu has **unquantified mg calcium** on the cited product page. Spinach calcium has low bioavailability; do not imply a single glass covers all needs. |
| Iodine | **150 micrograms/day** adult RDI. | Check fortified bread, fish/seafood, eggs and **iodised** table salt *when actually used*. | An iodised salt source does not justify adding salt without regard to sodium. Some organic or specialty bread is exempt from mandatory Australian iodine fortification. Avoid unmeasured kelp/seaweed supplements. |
| Sodium | Limit about **2,000 mg sodium/day** (~5 g salt), across all food. | No-added-salt tinned beans/tomatoes, lemon/herbs instead of more salt; check ready-to-eat fish and tamari panels. | Fish often contains sodium even in springwater; tofu, fortified drink and bread can also contribute. |
| Fish/seafood | Heart Foundation recommends **2–3 occasions/week**, commonly **150 g fresh fish** or **100 g canned fish** as a serve. | Count V6 A (120 g drained sardines/person), B (125 g RAW salmon/person: less after cooking), plus salmon dinners and non-app meals; rotate with tofu/lentils. | Do not count raw salmon grams as the same as cooked grams or assume 4 fish meals = four exact guideline serves. Pregnancy-specific mercury advice differs. |
| Nuts/seeds | A **small ~30 g handful unsalted nuts/day** can fit a heart-healthy pattern. | V6 currently includes ~10 g walnuts/lunch; add nuts in other meals **if desired and affordable**, not compulsorily on top of calories already sufficient. | Nut allergies, energy intake, and total nuts from breakfast/snacks all matter. |
| Plant protein | Legumes and tofu are encouraged within a diverse pattern. | Keep plant-based A/B/C/D options, but check dinner and breakfast protein *as full portions*, not just one headline ingredient. | No universal 30 g per-meal protein target is established as a human-lifespan maximum; physical activity and total intake matter. |

Primary reference links: [Australian recommended serves](https://www.eatforhealth.gov.au/food-essentials/how-much-do-we-need-each-day/recommended-number-serves-adults), [vegetable serving weights](https://www.eatforhealth.gov.au/food-essentials/five-food-groups/vegetables-and-legumes-beans), [fibre NRV](https://www.eatforhealth.gov.au/nutrient-reference-values/nutrients/dietary-fibre), [calcium NRV](https://www.eatforhealth.gov.au/nutrient-reference-values/nutrients/calcium), [iodine NRV](https://www.eatforhealth.gov.au/nutrient-reference-values/nutrients/iodine), [FSANZ bread iodine fortification](https://www.foodstandards.gov.au/consumer/food-fortification/iodine-fortification), [Heart Foundation sodium](https://www.heartfoundation.org.au/sodium-and-salt-converter), [Heart Foundation fish sizes](https://www.heartfoundation.org.au/blog/not-eating-enough-fish), [Heart Foundation 30 g nuts](https://www.heartfoundation.org.au/healthy-living/healthy-eating/fats-oils-and-heart-health).

## 3. Meal timing evidence, explicitly ranked

**Highest confidence:** Consistent, sufficient healthy dietary patterns, adequate sleep, not habitually eating heavy meals right before bed if that disrupts sleep/reflux, and meal-planning times compatible with real life. These are **practical conclusions**; direct longevity trials for exact 7:30am/12:30pm/6pm are unavailable.

**Moderate for metabolic *surrogates* (not longevity):** [2026 BMJ Medicine network meta-analysis](https://bmjmedicine.bmj.com/content/5/1/e001071) pooled **41 trials, 2,287 participants**, median trial eight weeks. Overall time-restricted eating improved several outcomes versus usual diet; earlier timing compared favourably with later timing for some measures. **No significant HbA1c advantage** established across some subtypes; fasting effects can include loss of lean mass. Evidence **does not identify the unique optimal hour**.

**Evidence against overclaiming:** The [2022 NEJM 12-month RCT](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2114833) of **139 adults** found adding an eating window to calorie restriction did not significantly improve weight loss at 12 months. [16-trial meal-frequency meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37964316/) found no discernible cardiometabolic advantage for more or fewer eating episodes (very-low-certainty evidence). [Eat for Health planning](https://www.eatforhealth.gov.au/eating-well/tips-eating-well/meal-planning) expressly allows **three meals without snacks** or smaller meals with snacks.

**Example, not prescription:** 07:30 breakfast, 12:30 lunch, 18:00 dinner = **10½-hour eating window** and **13½-hour overnight interval**, not 16:8. A 10:00 or 15:30 snack falls inside. If hungry, active, unwell, pregnant, a child or otherwise needing later intake, **eat and adjust**; avoid punishing missed times and avoid clock-triggered prompts to skip necessary food. A later dinner is not a moral/health failure.

## 4. Food safety: more consequential than a “longevity” spice

1. [NSW leftovers](https://www.foodauthority.nsw.gov.au/consumer/food-extra-care/leftovers): **shallow containers**, promptly into fridge/freezer (within **2 hours**), fridge **below 5°C**, ordinary refrigerated leftovers within **3 days**, cooked rice/pasta within **2 days**, frozen up to **3 months**, thaw in fridge and use within **24 hours**, **reheat until steaming** and rice/pasta only once. Danger-zone 2-hour/4-hour rule is cumulative; **not a licence to leave 20 hot dense bowls on a bench for two hours**.
2. [NSW temperature table](https://www.foodauthority.nsw.gov.au/consumer/food-safety-home/cooking-temperatures): chicken **74°C** at centre, fish **63°C**, reheated leftovers generally **60°C minimum** (the [home entertaining advice](https://www.foodauthority.nsw.gov.au/consumer/food-safety-home/entertaining-home) suggests **75°C when reheating leftovers**, a prudent target). Use a cleaned probe.
3. **At-risk populations:** [NSW low-immunity guidance](https://www.foodauthority.nsw.gov.au/consumer/food-risk-you/low-immunity) advises freshly cooked dishes and washed fresh fruit/vegetables, and only **one day** for promptly chilled leftovers. [FSANZ higher-risk foods](https://www.foodstandards.gov.au/consumer/prevention-of-foodborne-illness/Food-safety-for-vulnerable-populations) flag chilled hummus/tahini, some salads and uncooked frozen produce. **A normal healthy-adult freezer plan cannot be assumed safe for all vulnerable people.**
4. **Berries**: Follow product preparation directions; for higher-risk people, thoroughly cook frozen berries then cool safely before adding to smoothies/yoghurt. FSANZ's **85°C for one minute** is a *validated thermal-processing reference* in its [hepatitis A berry processing guidance](https://www.foodstandards.gov.au/publications/Guidance-for-Thermal-Inactivation-of-Hepatitis-A-virus-in-Berries), not a universal home-cooking rule for healthy people. Thawing alone does not deactivate viruses.
5. Never wash raw chicken (cross-contamination) and never presume smell/taste detects contaminated cooked rice. [FSANZ basics](https://www.foodstandards.gov.au/consumer/prevention-of-foodborne-illness/food-safety-basics).

## 5. Major outstanding software/data gaps and acceptance criteria

**P0 — next code/data effort:**

- **Structured nutrients:** download and map [AFCD Release 3](https://www.foodstandards.gov.au/science-data/food-nutrient-databases/afcd/data-files) profiles (1,588 Australian foods, up to 58 core nutrients); reconcile retail labels for fish, tofu, soy drinks and calcium-fortified alternatives. Provide *known/estimated/unknown*, serving convention, source/date and per-adult meal energy, protein, fibre, calcium, iron, sodium, iodine, vitamin D, B12, folate, potassium and omega-3 where data exist. **Do not display 0 for unknown** or extrapolate cooked nutrient weight from raw without a justified cooking yield.
- **Real Dubbo basket:** V6 $256.43 is a 35-line *first-shop estimate*, not all-day groceries. The planner's day/week quantities don't map synonyms (EVOO vs extra virgin olive oil, carrots vs carrot sticks), packaging, leftovers, rounding, or discounts. Build a canonical product/ingredient ID system plus separately displayed *food required*, *packages to buy* and *estimated/verified checkout*, including dietary variants and pantry carryover.
- **Actual cook test:** for each of 17 recipes, check 1-portion taste/texture and cooking duration, 4/5-serving measured yield, fridge load, frozen/thawed results, printed/phone readability, allergen labels and real substitution affordability. Historical planned meals **must not be marked cooked**.
- **Browser acceptance tests:** test on actual mobile touch device: recipe filtering, scrolling/focus, cooking modal with independent timers, scheduler time edits, Saturday/DST rollover, shopping checkbox storage, offline/unstable network and large-text/screen-reader operation. Unit/CI success is necessary but insufficient.
- **Food-safety UX:** make per-recipe temperature and cooling a prominent, non-dismissible checklist step; ensure timer countdowns don't act as an absolute guarantee of food safety (temperature/texture checks still required). Offer an opt-in higher-risk-food-safety warning mode rather than silent defaulting to rules inappropriate for vulnerable eaters.

**P1 — app convenience improvements:**

- **Realistic optional snacking** rather than a rigid five eating occasions/day. Prefer a customizable three-meal template plus optional snacks for new plans *after users choose whether to migrate existing defaults*. Don't delete existing saved schedules without permission.
- **Fish count caveat:** current default week schedules A/B fish lunches and an optional salmon dinner. The weekly view now counts fish **occasions** but cannot calculate actual cooked grams, since lunch B starts at **125 g raw**. Do not congratulate people on hitting a fish target solely from four fish occasions.
- **Personal needs without accounts:** user-adjustable dietary tags (dairy-free; verified GF only; nut/soy/egg/fish restrictions), number of adult servings, preferred meal slots and grocery shop; keep private browser data private and provide export/import to migrate devices.
- **Readability and provenance:** fresh scientific links from the page to specific source claims; consistent meal-type filters and safe warnings in cooking mode, not only buried in recipe text; avoid subjective 0–100 health scores.

**P2 — careful future research:**

- Food swaps can improve nutrient density, but **no evidence that a commercial fortified soy drink becomes worse merely by being processed**, or that a premium organic bean has higher longevity benefit. Focus on nutrient panel and food-group choices over processing dogma. [BMJ 2024 UPF umbrella review](https://www.bmj.com/content/384/bmj-2023-077310) found associations with adverse health but most of the evidence was observational with low/very-low certainty; [2024 BMJ cohort](https://www.bmj.com/content/385/bmj-2023-078476) found associations vary across subgroups. Treat sugar-loaded drinks and processed meat differently from a useful calcium-fortified soy milk.
- Avoid assuming omega-3 supplements, fasting mimetics, probiotics, expensive spices, CGMs, micronutrient mega-doses or biological-age testing automatically extend lifespan.
- **Measure success by** meals actually cooked/eaten, real food-bill totals, less avoidable waste, adequate daily food, tolerability and practicality over weeks. *Do not promise lifespan increments.*

## Bottom line — one high-value next experiment

Make **one real week** with the recipes already provided: three main meals each day, snack when useful, at least a few different plant-protein mains, regular oily fish where appropriate, two daily fruit portions and colourful vegetables at multiple meals. Keep the intended fortified calcium/iodine sources explicit. Log **actual food portions, any failed food-safety steps, food cost, hunger and enjoyment** without collecting unnecessary medical data. Use those observations and AFCD nutrient mapping to determine V7 changes, *not another invented 100/100 score*.
